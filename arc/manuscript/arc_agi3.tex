\begin{abstract}
We present the G\"odel--Kolmogorov Machine (GKM), an architecture that retains
verified executable solver components for interactive ARC-AGI-3. The central question
is how much new description each successive solution requires, given the solver
already retained. A coding language model proposes game- and
level-dependent programs. The implemented host promotes the first candidate that reaches
the exact next level and passes fresh execution, independent full-path replay,
taint/provenance, and containment checks. Older acquisition histories are distinguished
from later endpoint certification. Source cost and a free energy diagnostic
are recorded after that candidate is fixed; they do not rank candidates or decide
admission. The selected GPT-5.6 campaign replays 181 of 183 public levels
($98.9071\%$ raw coverage; $98.1166\%$ Competition-Mode score). The source audit
admits 171 exact winning checkpoints. A computable conditional-AST novelty proxy
decreases in 64 of 141 comparable marginals, including 21 half-or-more drops;
13 of those coincide with direct static call sites to unchanged earlier legs. We publish
the conditional marginal profiles for all 25 games and examine concrete source
structure at their dips, including direct and indirect helper reuse and a compact
literal action program. Source-growth counters are supporting accounting and are
not substituted for conditional complexity. These are source and replay observations; runtime call
execution and causal search savings are unmeasured. We use pushout attachments and
computable structure functions as concise specifications of retention and accounting,
while distinguishing this mathematical background from the implemented system.
Public-solution replay does not measure discovery cost or held-out generalization.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} G\"odel--Kolmogorov Machine; ARC-AGI-3; Kolmogorov
structure function; free energy; inverse colimit; cofibration; PowerPlay; artificial
curiosity; self-improving coding agent; program synthesis.

\section{Introduction}
ARC-AGI-3 is an interactive benchmark in which objects, action semantics, mechanics,
and goals must be inferred through interaction~\cite{chollet2019measure,arcagi3}.
This paper studies the marginal description cost of acquiring another verified
ability: given the executable solver already retained, how much new code or data
is needed for the next successful program? A reusable component is described once;
later applications may need only a binding and residual instructions. A new mechanism
may instead require substantial new source. Alternation between those situations
motivates the marginal-complexity sawtooth examined here. Total source size,
conditional novelty, endpoint coverage, and discovery effort answer different questions.

The G\"odel--Kolmogorov Machine (GKM) separates a fallible coding proposer from a
fixed verifier. The proposer works from the highest clean promotion and may add
reusable routines, level-specific bindings, search procedures, or literal action
programs. The host first tries a bounded set of incumbent-leg calls, then accepts
the first proposed candidate that reaches the exact next boundary and passes the
execution, full-path replay, provenance, taint, and containment gates. Its complete
solver tree becomes the parent for the next level. Description cost is measured
only after this choice. The frozen campaign did not select among successful
candidates by complexity or free energy.

The contribution is the verified architecture and its inspectable evidence:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.1em]
\item A retained solver archive with exact-boundary admission, independent replay,
and explicit separation between proposer claims and host verification.
\item A frozen 25-game release covering 181/183 public levels, with endpoint replay
separated from the narrower set of 171 exact winning-source checkpoints.
\item Conditional marginal profiles at exact winning boundaries across all 25 games,
with a source-history audit of their rises and falls and static call sites to unchanged
earlier components. Supporting source-growth counters are separated from these
marginals, runtime call execution, and causal effects on search effort.
\end{enumerate}
The complete uniform histories \texttt{wa30} and \texttt{ls20}, together with the
accounting contrast in \texttt{dc22}, illustrate both the marginal patterns and
the evidence needed before attributing a trough to reuse.

The title's mathematical terms describe the specification and accounting vocabulary.
Pushouts state how unchanged interfaces can be extended; structure functions organize
verified depth against a declared description coordinate. Their standard properties
are background, not new mathematical results of this empirical paper. The separate
theoretical paper \emph{Pushout Attachments and Conditional Complexity of Executable
Models}~\cite{pushoutmodels} treats the conditional-complexity framework. Here the
observations do not establish a theorem that categorical structure causes compression
or improved performance. A conditional enumerability argument, stated in
Appendix~\ref{sec:compute}, likewise provides no practical rate guarantee for the
implemented LLM proposer.

\section{Architectural Lineage}
GKM takes its intellectual starting point from Schmidhuber's G\"odel machine:
a proposed self-rewrite crosses an external verification gate before adoption
\cite{schmidhuber2007godel}. The distinction is substantial. A G\"odel machine
requires a formal proof of expected-utility improvement under its axioms; GKM checks
a finite behavioral claim by fresh execution and replay. Its certificate supports
that admitted behavior, not optimality or correctness in all future states.

PowerPlay searches for new task--solver modifications while preserving previously
acquired skills~\cite{schmidhuber2013powerplay}. GKM uses the externally ordered ARC
levels as tasks, retains each successful solver, and prefers incumbent-leg trials
before requesting new code. This is a curriculum and proposal-order correspondence;
the frozen harness did not optimize the simplest successful modification. A deeper
full-path replay re-establishes every earlier depth, without requiring identity with
the historical action path for that depth.

Schmidhuber's artificial curiosity rewards attainable learning or compression progress
\cite{schmidhuber2010curiosity}. It motivates future probe-allocation experiments.
The reported campaign did not implement a free energy progress reward or test a
curiosity-based controller. Detailed auxiliary proposer roles and subsequent control
designs are separated in Appendix~\ref{sec:auxiliary}.

\subsection{Interface and proposer}
The game-independent local interface exposes only
\begin{center}
\texttt{reset()}$\to$\emph{frame}, \quad
\texttt{step(action)}$\to$\emph{frame}, \quad
\texttt{frame()} \text{ (raw $64\times64$ grid)}, \\
\texttt{levels\_completed}, \quad \texttt{actions}, \quad
\texttt{terminal()}, \quad \texttt{clone()}.
\end{center}
No interface field names objects, mechanics, or goals. The proposer nevertheless carries
broad priors from pretraining and from the prompt: persistent objects, barriers,
controllable and autonomous entities, sparse reward, and the value of active probes. It
works in a disposable directory seeded from the highest clean promotion, may execute
cloned environments, and writes a \texttt{solve(env)} program.

Auxiliary proposer roles and later control designs are described in
Appendix~\ref{sec:auxiliary}; they are not additional evidence for the frozen score.

The harness treats proposer output as untrusted. Recognized private-runtime and hidden-
source access is blocked before execution, and tainted workspaces are rejected before
replay or promotion. Earlier attempts to access a private \texttt{env.\_game} object are
preserved as dirty WIP and excluded from all reported artifacts. The boundary is an
implemented path and lexical detector, not an information-flow theorem; the central
point is that prompt compliance is not used as the verification gate.

\subsection{Admission and retention}
The promoted workspace separates reusable and level-specific source.
\texttt{legs.py} contains shared components, \texttt{players.py} contains thin level
entry points, and \texttt{solve.py} dispatches by observed progress. The host first
tries a bounded set of single calls to public incumbent legs. Only after those attempts
fail does the proposer receive the level. Its contract asks it to factor new behavior
through retained legs and to add only the missing objects, maps, and glue. Section~\ref{sec:category} gives a source specification of these bindings.

The operational cycle is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.1em]
\item seed from the highest clean artifact;
\item recover any clean winning source interrupted before promotion, then mechanically
run bounded trials that each call one public incumbent leg;
\item propose a program when the incumbent-leg trials do not advance;
\item compile and execute the linked candidate in the restricted workspace;
\item stop at the first candidate that reaches the exact next boundary and passes the
independent full-path replay, taint, provenance, and containment gates;
\item promote its complete solver tree and archive the evidence, making that tree the
diagram available at the next level;
\item record retained source size and the winning-iteration workspace charge after
candidate fixation.
\end{enumerate}
Known failed compositions are keyed by level and library hash, so a restart need not
repeat the same expensive search. A proposer-mutated checkpoint remains an untrusted
path candidate until the harness reproduces it.

This cycle states the implemented admission contract. Frozen endpoint certification
separately rechecked source execution, exact-boundary path replay, taint, source
identity, and parent provenance under the receipt-bound verifier. It does not establish
that every older acquisition used the later containment controls. The inherited
paid-debrief histories and later no-debrief setting are distinguished below; later
container-role designs in Appendix~\ref{sec:auxiliary} are not historical guarantees.

Proposal instructions encourage reuse and small glue. This can influence which
candidate appears first, but it is not a measured complexity ranking. Admission is
first success under the stated gates, independent of source cost and free energy. The
later frozen configuration set \texttt{--debrief-policy=never}; older inherited source
histories include paid debriefs. The audit uses pre-debrief snapshots where available
and excludes the three unauthenticated auto-solve reconstructions described in
Section~\ref{sec:source-audit}.

Allocation among games was a separate choice. It used clean retry history and a
heuristic based on retained core source byte count. It did not use
$F_{\mathrm{diag}}$. This allocation heuristic is distinct from candidate admission.

\section{Conditional Marginal Complexity and Source Accounting}
\label{sec:accounting}
\subsection{The marginal-complexity question}
\label{sec:marginal-definition}
For successive exact winning programs $P_{k-1},P_k$, the ideal conditional
description coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 m_k^{\mathrm{ideal}}=K(P_k\mid P_{k-1}).
 \label{eq:ideal-marginal}
\end{equation}
It asks for the shortest description of the chosen next program when the earlier
one is available. It is not the difference of their unconditional complexities,
their difference in source size, or the time spent discovering either program.
Nor does it assert that $P_k$ is the shortest solver for the new level. Ideal $K$
is uncomputable; our empirical analysis therefore uses a declared conditional
source-novelty proxy, not numerical estimates of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ideal-marginal}.

Let $A(P)$ be the multiset of normalized top-level Python AST statements in $P$.
For consecutive exact winning states we measure
\begin{equation}
 M_k=\left|\operatorname{zlib}_9\!\left(
 A(P_k)\setminus_{\mathrm{multi}}A(P_{k-1})\right)\right|.
 \label{eq:conditional-ast}
\end{equation}
The unmatched, location-free AST dumps are emitted in sorted filename order and
current top-level statement order within each file, each prefixed by its eight-byte
big-endian length, then compressed with zlib level~9. Literal matches in the
preceding program are omitted; a same-size replacement is charged. $M_1$ uses an
empty AST dictionary and describes initial construction, rather than change from
an authenticated prior promotion. Unknown source and missing predecessors yield
unknown marginals, not zeros, and comparisons never bridge those gaps.

This measurement expresses the paper's central empirical question in compressed
bytes. It is representation-dependent and omits a full reconstruction protocol,
including ordering and dictionary-reference metadata. It is neither a complete
prefix-code length nor a calibrated estimate or upper bound for ideal $K$.
It also cannot recognize all algorithmically equivalent rewrites. The encoding
is fixed throughout the selected campaign.

A new mechanism can raise the conditional marginal because a new routine must be
described. A later variant can lower it if retained machinery suffices with small
bindings or residual instructions. A further unfamiliar mechanism can raise it
again. Repetition produces the sawtooth hypothesis, not a requirement on every
game or every adjacent pair. Different layouts can share mechanics; similar
layouts can require new logic. The level's appearance alone does not establish
how much is reusable or whether the proposer found it. The source and call-site
audit is therefore needed alongside the marginal curve.

\subsection{Supporting source-growth counters and post-admission diagnostics}
For a source file $f$, $d(f)$ counts nonblank, noncomment lines plus the number of
elements in Python list, tuple, set, and dictionary literals in its AST. A long
one-line action list therefore incurs a literal surcharge. For retained library and
player sources $\ell_k,p_k$ after promotion $k$, define
\begin{align}
 D_k&=d(\ell_k)+d(p_k),\label{eq:retained-description}\\
 G_k&=\Pos{d(\ell_k)-d(\ell_{k-1})}
       +\Pos{d(p_k)-d(p_{k-1})},\qquad k\ge2.\label{eq:marginal-c}
\end{align}
Here $D_k$ is retained size and $G_k$ is recomputed positive growth between adjacent
retained states. For the historical checkpoint field \texttt{marginal\_C}, however,
the producer compares the final source with the working files
$\bar\ell_k,\bar p_k$ saved at the start of the winning iteration:
\begin{equation}
 C_k^{\mathrm{recorded}}
 =\Pos{d(\ell_k)-d(\bar\ell_k)}
  +\Pos{d(p_k)-d(\bar p_k)}.
 \label{eq:recorded-workspace-charge}
\end{equation}
That workspace can already contain source from earlier work, so its baseline need
not be the preceding promotion. $C_k^{\mathrm{recorded}}$ and $G_k$ consequently
need not agree. The earlier manuscript incorrectly identified them; the
\texttt{dc22} example in Section~\ref{sec:dc22} distinguishes their actual baselines.
A difference is not by itself a harness defect or an invalid solver.

These quantities use source-accounting units, not bits, tokens, elapsed time, or
Kolmogorov complexity. Within one file, changes are netted before taking the positive
part. Comments, identifier lengths, the fixed dispatcher, temporary probes, inference,
and environment interactions are outside this coordinate. The all-game table and
source-counter plots preserve the recorded workspace-local charges. We report $G_k$ only
where both adjacent retained states are authenticated; the recorded-charge table
does not impute missing $G_k$ values. After fixing a winning source and exact
boundary, the harness reported
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathrm{diag}}(k)=-k+0.02\sum_{j\le k}C_j^{\mathrm{recorded}}.
 \label{eq:implemented-diagnostic}
\end{equation}
No admission, rejection, or candidate-ranking branch consumed this diagnostic.

For any real sequence, the elementary telescoping identity
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=1}^{n}\Pos{x_k-x_{k-1}}
 =x_n-x_0+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Pos{x_{k-1}-x_k}
 \label{eq:positive-variation}
\end{equation}
relates cumulative positive growth to endpoint growth and subsequent removals.
Applied separately from the first authenticated promotion onward, it describes
$\sum_{k=2}^{n}G_k$.
It does not imply that the workspace-local $\sum C_k^{\mathrm{recorded}}$ telescopes
to retained size, nor that either quantity measures the complete cost of discovery.

\subsection{What the structure function contributes}
For finite data $x$, the classical structure function
\begin{equation}
 h_x(\alpha)=\min\{\log |S|:x\in S,\ K(S)\le\alpha\}
 \label{eq:classical-structure-function}
\end{equation}
separates ideal model description from residual data description
\cite{kolmogorov1965,solomonoff1964,vereshchagin2004,live2008}.
Ideal prefix complexity $K$ is uncomputable and machine-dependent up to an additive
constant. Neither our line/literal coordinate nor compressed AST novelty estimates
$K$ with a calibrated error bound. Both are computable, representation-dependent
measurements. A frozen encoding permits reproducible comparisons within a lineage;
it does not remove representation dependence.

For a finite evaluated archive $\mathcal P$, with recorded verified depths $V(s)$
and computed descriptions $D_\kappa(s)$, let $R(s)=m-V(s)$ in a game with $m$ levels.
An empirical inverse loss--complexity profile is
\begin{equation}
 C^*_{\mathcal P,\kappa}(r)
 =\inf\{D_\kappa(s):s\in\mathcal P,\ R(s)\le r\},\qquad
 \widehat C_{\mathcal P,\kappa}(v)=C^*_{\mathcal P,\kappa}(m-v).
 \label{eq:structure-function}
\end{equation}
With $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$, this finite profile is computable by comparison of
the evaluated entries. No such computability is asserted for an arbitrary effective
set of unevaluated programs. This specializes the structure-function framework
of~\cite{losscomplexity}.
It organizes retained description against a verified frontier. The conditional
coordinate in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ideal-marginal} then asks what additional description
is required at the next win, with the retained program supplied as context;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-ast} provides the empirical novelty profile. A difference
between two values of $D_\kappa$ is not substituted for that conditional coordinate.
A promoted history supplies feasible points, hence upper bounds on the best size
within any proposal set containing those points. Because the campaign retains one
first-success lineage rather than a population of alternatives, it does not estimate
the optimum profile or demonstrate movement along its lower envelope.

The associated scalar envelope is the standard expression
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{\mathcal P,\kappa}(\lambda)
 =\inf_{s\in\mathcal P}\{R(s)+\lambda D_\kappa(s)\}
 =\inf_r\{r+\lambda C^*_{\mathcal P,\kappa}(r)\},\quad\lambda>0.
 \label{eq:model-dual}
\end{equation}
This background organizes a possible comparison among retained alternatives
\cite{losscomplexity,grunwald2007mdl}; it is not the implemented admission rule.
For two candidates, comparing their values reduces algebraically to
$R(s)-R(s')>\lambda(D_\kappa(s')-D_\kappa(s))$. A future controller could test
such a local preference, with a declared coding and weight. No fixed weight is
validated across games with different horizons and mechanics. The present audit
uses source novelty and call-site evidence directly; it requires no thermodynamic
sampling model, partition function, or fitted free energy dynamics.

\section{Pushout Specification of Retained Program Growth}
\label{sec:category}
A finite source snapshot can be represented by a $\Sigma$-typed incidence graph
whose nodes include definitions, interfaces, call sites, and dispatch bindings.
Let $\Syn$ denote these finite presentations with type-preserving graph maps.
For a fixed presheaf-style graph signature, finite pushouts along monomorphisms
preserve the incumbent inclusion~\cite{lack2005adhesive,riehl2016category}.
We call these inclusions \emph{cofibrations}. This is a chosen persistence
convention for the source specification, not a new category-theoretic result or a
claim that every Python edit is injective.

For incumbent $X_{k-1}$, interface $A_k$, new component $B_k$, interface inclusion
$i_k$, and binding map $a_k$, the linked presentation is the pushout
\begin{equation}
 P_k=X_{k-1}\sqcup_{A_k}B_k.
 \label{eq:inverse-colimit-attachment}
\end{equation}
The phrase \emph{inverse colimit} in the title means the colimit of the
inverse-shaped span $X_{k-1}\leftarrow A_k\rightarrow B_k$: assigning degree one
to $A_k$ and degree zero to the other objects makes both arrows lower degree.
It is an ordinary pushout, distinct from an inverse limit. We use \emph{pushout
attachment} below to avoid that ambiguity.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge,row sep=large]
A_k \arrow[r,hook,"i_k"] \arrow[d,"a_k"']
 & B_k \arrow[d,"b_k"] \\
X_{k-1} \arrow[r,hook,"j_k"']
 & P_k=X_{k-1}\sqcup_{A_k}B_k
\end{tikzcd}
\caption{Source-level pushout specification. The interface and binding identify how
new code uses retained definitions. Pushout stability preserves $j_k$ when $i_k$
is monic; replay supplies a separate finite behavioral check.}
\label{fig:inverse-colimit-pushout}
\end{figure}

The universal property says that compatible maps from incumbent and component
factor uniquely through the linked presentation. This is the standard definition
of a pushout~\cite{maclane1998,riehl2016category}; the practical content here is
the source record identifying the interface, unchanged definitions, and binding.
Several components can be attached successively when their interfaces map into
the partial presentation. Strictly additive histories give a sequential colimit;
rewrites of existing definitions require separate treatment.

For a finite replay suite $T$, write $P\simeq_TQ$ when both programs satisfy the
same declared depth obligations. A proposed acquisition and optional refactor may
then be described as
\begin{equation}
 X_{k-1}\hookrightarrow P_k\simeq_{T_k}X_k.
 \label{eq:cofibration-refactor-factorization}
\end{equation}
A source diff must witness the inclusion before calling an edit cofibrational.
Agreement on $T$ is finite validation, not semantic equivalence on all inputs.
The generic harness has a replay-checked debrief facility; the later frozen campaign
configuration set \texttt{--debrief-policy=never}. Older inherited histories include
paid debriefs; their exact acquisition sources require separate pre-debrief evidence. Python linking realizes the program; the host does
not construct graph-category objects or compute Kan extensions.

The source specification explains why a retained routine need not be redescribed
in every later player. For a chosen two-part encoding, a new attachment can be
represented by code for new definitions, bindings, and explicit decoding metadata.
All three costs belong in that encoding. We assume no general $O(\log k)$ metadata
bound, and the line/literal metric is not such a prefix code. The conditional-AST
statistic in Section~\ref{sec:source-audit} makes one concrete dictionary convention
reproducible without invoking an ideal-code equality.

Verification has a separate elementary description. A replay-valid path to depth
$k$ can be truncated at first passage to any $j\le k$. With $\rho_{k,j}$ denoting
that restriction,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{j,i}\circ\rho_{k,j}=\rho_{k,i}\quad(i\le j\le k).
 \label{eq:certificate-coherence}
\end{equation}
This is an inverse system of depth certificates. It re-establishes earlier depths
along the newly validated path; its restrictions need not equal the historically
stored earlier paths. The observation neither establishes that source pushouts
are behavioral pushouts nor proves that a particular call site executed. Those
questions require additional evidence beyond the graph specification.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The central object of this study is the conditional marginal description of a
newly verified solver, not the growth of its source files. A retained library can
continue to grow while the extra description needed for another variant falls.
Conversely, a solver can reuse an unchanged routine and still need a larger
marginal description because its route data or residual control become more
complicated. Neither reuse nor successful retention requires a decreasing curve.

The measured $M_k$ profiles show repeated rises and falls in several games, with
\GKMDecreases{} decreases among \GKMComparable{} comparable marginals. Alternation
between learning new machinery and applying retained machinery is the proposed
explanation for a marginal-complexity sawtooth. It is supported most specifically
when a decrease coincides with source evidence of a retained call: the audit finds
\GKMHardReuse{} static unchanged-leg cases, including \GKMCoupledWitnesses{} with
half-or-more novelty drops. In \texttt{ls20}, for example, the L2 drop accompanies
a static call to unchanged \texttt{follow\_cardinal\_runs}; later route literals
add novelty while that leg remains unchanged. A matched experiment would still
be needed to attribute a reduction in discovery effort to access to the library.

The source shows reuse in all four \texttt{dc22}/\texttt{wa30} dips examined here.
The new \texttt{dc22} routines call unchanged \texttt{walk\_segments} and
\texttt{toggle\_control}; the new \texttt{wa30} routines call unchanged \texttt{walk}
and \texttt{interact}. These are concrete call paths through an added wrapper,
which the direct-call test excludes. The corrected \texttt{dc22} L5 dip is modest,
but its unchanged helpers remain available and referenced.

There is also a concrete alternative source pattern. In \texttt{cn04} L3,
$M$ falls from 337 to 245 bytes and the new player is a 23-action literal loop
whose only call is \texttt{env.step}. That player bypasses the retained solver
legs. Thus the observed dips include both reuse through retained helpers and a
compact action program; Table~\ref{tab:reuse-cases} identifies which occurs in
each examined case. This distinction follows from the winning source, rather
than from the scalar curve alone.

Admission remains execution and replay; the marginal curves analyze the resulting
first-success lineage. The structure-function account separates retained description
from conditional novelty, while the source-counter appendix reconciles working and
promoted baselines. The concrete source cases explain how the retained program
and the new residual code contribute to each measured marginal.

\subsection{Limits and controlled follow-up}
The 181/183 result is replay of solutions on the public set. It is not a held-out
generalization estimate or an end-to-end discovery score. Clone-enabled interaction,
failed proposals, temporary programs, and inference cost exceed the stored replay
paths; historical token and compute logs are not uniform enough to reconstruct the
full discovery bill. The campaign is one adaptive chronological run, not repeated
randomized trials. Its shared protocol does not hold model, effort, and budget fixed
across all attempted frontiers. Retained components may reduce search effort, but
this remains a hypothesis without matched-model, matched-budget library ablations.

Source size and compressed AST novelty depend on the chosen representation, with
no calibrated relation to ideal $K$. Static analysis can include an unexecuted call
site, and exact winning-source coverage is incomplete for ten replay-verified
boundaries. Deterministic path replay checks finite admitted behavior, not robustness
under perturbations. Taint and containment checks enforce the declared boundary but
are not a formal noninterference proof. The categorical source specification adds no
behavioral guarantee beyond the recorded checks. The conditional enumeration argument
in Appendix~\ref{sec:compute} assumes fair search or a uniform positive conditional
success bound at every reached stage; neither property is established for the
historical LLM campaign.

A controlled follow-up should instrument actual calls and compare library access
under the same model and discovery budget. Multiple valid candidates at a depth
would permit direct study of the empirical loss--description frontier. Free energy
ranking, curiosity-based probe allocation, and broader open-ended growth are proposed
extensions; none is used to explain the frozen score causally.

\section{Conclusion}
GKM studies the marginal description needed to extend a retained verified solver.
The selected GPT-5.6 release replays 181/183 public levels, scoring $98.1166\%$ in
Competition Mode with 7001 stored path actions and 7069 official API calls. At the
171 exact winning-source checkpoints, the conditional-AST novelty proxy decreases
in \GKMDecreases{} of \GKMComparable{} comparable steps, including \GKMSharpDrops{}
half-or-more drops and \GKMCoupledWitnesses{} sharp static call-site cases. The
complete marginal profiles expose sawtooth patterns and exceptions, while the
source-growth counters provide separate supporting accounting. The corrected
\texttt{dc22} profile is nonmonotone despite increasing promoted-source growth.

Admission uses first success at the exact next level under execution, replay,
provenance, taint, and containment checks. Complexity and free energy do not rank
candidates. The conditional-complexity and structure-function framework formulates
the question; the computable source proxy supplies reproducible observations,
not ideal Kolmogorov values. Actual call execution, causal search savings, full
discovery cost, and private-set performance require further measurement.

\section{Conditional Search Completeness}
\label{sec:compute}
Let $\mathcal A_B$ be the retained archive along one run after cumulative compute
budget $B$, and $V_B(g)=\max_{s\in\mathcal A_B}V_g(s)$. Retention makes this
frontier nondecreasing in $B$. That observation says nothing about the waiting time
to a new promotion.

For context, standard universal-search reasoning~\cite{levin1973} gives a
conditional existence statement. Suppose finite candidate programs are effectively
enumerable, finite successful replay is recognizable, and the scheduler fairly
dovetails candidate index, runtime, and action allowance. A finite winning program
then has a finite index and finite successful execution bound, so the scheduler
eventually tests and recognizes it. For a deterministic resettable game with $q$
finite actions and a winning trace of length $L$, breadth-first trace enumeration
uses at most $\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}q^\ell$ tests. Literal traces suffice; the argument
does not depend on categorical structure or complexity regularization.

A corresponding stochastic bound requires more than pointwise full support. At
stage $k$, assume every trial, conditional on the entire past and continued failure,
has success probability at least a fixed $p_k>0$. If $\tau_k$ is the number of trials
to advance one stage, successive conditioning gives
\begin{equation}
 \Prb(\tau_k>n)\le(1-p_k)^n,\qquad \E[\tau_k]\le p_k^{-1}.
 \label{eq:geometric-stage}
\end{equation}
A finite chain then completes almost surely; the union bound gives
$\Prb(\sum_k\tau_k>\sum_kn_k)\le\sum_k(1-p_k)^{n_k}$.
These are elementary consequences of the assumptions, not measured properties of
the proposer. Positive probability of each finite string alone does not establish
a history-uniform $p_k$. The frozen campaign neither ran the fair enumerator nor
estimated these bounds, and its fixed action and compute limits remain binding.

\section{Auxiliary Proposer Roles and Subsequent Designs}
\label{sec:auxiliary}
The following role descriptions document exploratory methods and later designs.
They do not replace the frozen release protocol or add results to its 181/183 score.

For persistent exploratory frontiers, the development workflow also introduced an independent
observation-only side expert. This was a role escalation, not an
\texttt{ultra}-effort setting: the exploratory implementation reassigned an in-session
audit subagent whose model/effort field was not exposed. The expert received a private
copy of the exact parent and admitted clean same-frontier WIP, pursued a question
deliberately distinct from the live proposer, and experimented only through the public
Arena interface. It shared the host process/filesystem boundary, so its workspace was
taint-checked at every Arena launch and it could write only to the private copy.
Findings returned through the session were hypotheses, not promotions. Any path, patch,
or abstraction derived from them still required exact-parent provenance, a clean taint
verdict, and fresh replay before admission to the solver lineage.

The planned contiguous replication makes that tactic scheduler-controlled and
game-independent. After medium, high, xhigh, and the first max turn all end as clean
no-progress at one exact frontier, otherwise-idle capacity may run one independent
side expert (\(n=5\)); failure of the subsequent max reset/continuation pair permits
two distinct complexity assignments (\(n\geq7\)). Assignment classes are mechanism induction, state
representation, exact planning, and prefix compression, never game names or remembered
solutions. The scheduler allocates auxiliary capacity but does not author a
game-specific brief: each assignment must bind an authenticated same-frontier
\texttt{NATIVE\_SIDECAR\_REQUEST} emitted by the native proposer or a request
inside an admitted supervisory handoff. Manual, scheduler-authored, stale,
cross-frontier, and substituted requests are rejected. The contiguous role uses a minimal container input manifest, a separately
pinned model/effort, journaled usage, and quarantine-only output. These prospective
controls must not be read backward as properties of the exploratory sidecars.
At the same complexity-gated stage, the contiguous design may schedule a distinct
supervisory proposer. It receives only an immutable, hash-bound allowlist of the exact
frontier, the exact-parent and selected admitted-WIP solver-source snapshots, cited
native-proposer observations, and admitted side-expert evidence. Its
schema-validated \texttt{SUPERVISORY\_HANDOFF} must state the unresolved obligation,
cite the observations on which each hypothesis depends, propose bounded falsification
tests, record rejected alternatives and caveats, and include a Socratic challenge to
its own recommendation. It may include a typed same-frontier sidecar request, but that
request does not allocate capacity and remains subject to the deterministic scheduler.
The role cannot choose the game, effort, concurrency, WIP
policy, or promotion, and cannot mutate a solver. Its output remains quarantined until
the native proposer reproduces every cited source observation through the public
interface and the host verifier admits the resulting candidate by ordinary replay. At
most one such role is active per
frontier; a later round requires newly admitted evidence or a complete scheduler-defined
reset/continuation pair, rather than a retry-count increment alone. Thus the deterministic
scheduler,
native proposer, side expert, supervisory proposer, and host verifier have separate
receipt-bound authorities; every winning boundary must disclose whether its acquisition
attempt was exposed to an admitted supervisory handoff.
The clean retry count is the single operational complexity coordinate for both
medium-to-max effort escalation and max-plus-sidecar role escalation. It measures
repeated failure of stronger searches at an unchanged verified boundary; it is not
presented as an estimator of Kolmogorov complexity.


These role and scheduler designs are distinct from free energy candidate ranking.
Their existence supplies no controlled evidence of improved discovery efficiency.
