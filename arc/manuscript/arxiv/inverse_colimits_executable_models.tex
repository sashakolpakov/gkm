\begin{abstract}
We study modular extensions of executable models as pushouts of finite typed
presentations.  Raw syntax is represented in the pointwise-finite part of a
presheaf slice, and a module with interface is a span whose boundary map is
monic.  Adhesivity makes the induced base map monic and recovers the interface
as a pullback.  For each base, chosen pushouts define a linking functor from the
category of such attachments to the category of pointed cofibration
extensions.  The degenerate-attachment functor is fully faithful and right
adjoint to linking, identifying pointed extensions with a reflective
subcategory of attachments; in the ambient syntax category, finite coproducts
describe parallel attachment.  A semantics functor yields a comparison from
the pushout of component behaviors to the behavior of the syntactic pushout,
whose invertibility gives a sufficient semantic monicity criterion.  Effective
linking bounds the conditional prefix complexity of an extension by that of
its complete attachment code.  Under the stated canonicalization and
loss-invariance hypotheses, unrestricted attachment codes and extension codes
induce the same loss--complexity profiles up to additive constants, and these
profiles admit an exact scalar envelope.  The framework separates syntactic
gluing, semantic exactness, ideal Kolmogorov complexity, and operational
description length, and shows that any nontrivial modular bias must come from
restrictions on the attachment grammar.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}
Adhesive category; cofibration; executable model; Kolmogorov complexity;
minimum description length; pushout.

\section{Introduction}

Finite executable presentations are often enlarged by binding a new module to
an existing presentation along a typed interface.  We represent such an
extension by a span
\[
 X\xleftarrow{u}A\xrightarrow{i}B
\]
and its pushout \(P=X\sqcup_A B\).  Here \(X\) is the base, \(A\) is the
interface, and \(B\) is the module to be attached.  Requiring the boundary map
\(i\) to be monic ensures that the induced map \(X\to P\) preserves the base
as a subpresentation.

Raw typed presentations are placed in a presheaf slice
\(\Syn=\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}}/T\), with
\(\Syn_{\mathrm f}\) denoting its pointwise-finite objects.  We call its
monomorphisms cofibrations.  This is only a choice of distinguished attachment
maps: no weak equivalences, lifting axioms, or model-category structure are
assumed.  Since presheaf slices are adhesive, pushouts along these maps
preserve monomorphisms and are also pullbacks~\cite{lack,toposes}.  Thus an
attachment preserves the base and has exact syntactic overlap.  These
categorical facts are standard; their role here is to specify precisely what
modular growth guarantees.

Syntactic persistence does not imply executability or behavioral persistence.
A language-specific checker need not accept every ambient pushout, so
admissibility is an explicit condition.  Likewise, for a semantics functor
\(\Run:\Exec\to\Beh\), applying \(\Run\) to an admissible attachment produces
a canonical comparison
\[
 \Run(X)\sqcup_{\Run(A)}\Run(B)\longrightarrow\Run(P).
\]
We call the attachment semantically exact when this map is invertible.  Under
pushout stability of monomorphisms in \(\Beh\), semantic exactness and
monicity of \(\Run(i)\) imply that \(\Run(X)\to\Run(P)\) is monic.  The
comparison therefore isolates the application-specific proof obligation
instead of conflating it with syntactic gluing.

For each base \(X\), we organize attachment spans and pointed cofibration
extensions into categories.  Chosen pushouts give a linking functor from
attachments to extensions, left adjoint to the fully faithful functor that
regards an extension as a degenerate attachment.  Hence extensions form a
reflective subcategory of attachments.  The effective object maps underlying
this ambient reflection provide the two coding directions used below;
together with canonicalization and loss invariance, they yield the profile
comparison up to additive shifts.  When degenerate cells are excluded, the
reflection need not restrict to the allowed grammar, and attachment syntax can
impose a genuine modular bias.

The contribution is the resulting compatibility chain.  An admissible
monic-boundary attachment yields a persistent syntactic extension; under the
stated behavioral hypotheses, semantic exactness transports that persistence
to behavior; and effective linking transports an attachment description to a
conditional description of the extension.  The categorical ingredients and
the scalar envelope are standard
in isolation.  We identify the attachment reflection and its effective coding
consequence so that none of admissibility, semantic preservation, ideal
Kolmogorov complexity, or a computable code is asserted to imply the others.

Pushout-based gluing relates the construction to adhesive graph transformation
~\cite{lack,toposes} and to categorical treatments of open systems by
structured cospans~\cite{baezcourser}.  Unlike structured cospans, which
compose open systems along boundary objects, our attachment category fixes a
base and treats an interface span as an object; the reflector retains only its
linked pointed extension.  The selection layer is a conditional restriction
of algorithmic loss--complexity and individual rate--distortion profiles
~\cite{vereshchagin,vereshchagin2010}, with minimum description length
providing the operational coding interpretation~\cite{rissanen}.  Executable
world-model formalisms motivate the presentation discipline in Section~2
~\cite{schmidhuber1991,schmidhuber2004,steunebrink2013}; the categorical
construction is not attributed to those works.

\section{Executable model presentations}

\subsection{The operational criterion}

\begin{definition}[Executable presentation]
Fix effective alphabets of actions \(\mathcal A\), observations \(\mathcal O\),
rewards \(\mathcal W\), patterns, values, and guards, together with a decidable
type-and-interface checker and one global effective interpreter \(I\).  An
\emph{executable presentation} is a finite hierarchy \(M\) of guarded rules
accepted by the checker, with effectively encoded configuration and goal
spaces.  The interpreter induces a computably enumerable backward relation
\[
 R_M^-\subseteq
 \operatorname{Goal}_M\times\operatorname{Goal}_M^*
\]
and an effective forward-chaining relation.  In an action-conditioned
specialization, the latter is the partial computable map
\[
 F_M:\operatorname{Conf}_M\times\mathcal A
       \rightharpoonup
       \operatorname{Conf}_M\times\mathcal O\times\mathcal W
\]
and may be iterated on its predicted configuration to form a rollout.  The
backward relation interprets a consequent as a goal whose antecedents become
subgoals.  Rule bodies may call other named rules through typed references.
\end{definition}

Acceptance by the checker is a syntactic executability condition, not a
termination or usefulness claim.  An ill-posed input or a nonterminating
computation has no output, and a later semantic loss may assign it infinite
loss.

\subsection{A typed presheaf category}

Let \(\mathcal C\) be a finite computably presented category whose objects
describe the syntactic
sorts---for example rule nodes, antecedent and consequent ports, guards,
state slots, and hierarchical references---and whose arrows describe their
incidences.  Fix a type-and-attribute presheaf \(T\), together with effective
codes for the attributes occurring in finite objects such that equality,
typing, incidence, and naturality of finite codes are decidable.  Raw typed
presentations form the slice
\[
 \Syn=\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}}/T.
\]
Write \(\Syn_{\mathrm f}\) for the full subcategory whose underlying presheaves
are pointwise finite.  Literal names and attributes that must be preserved are
encoded in the map to \(T\), rather than left as informal decorations.

A presentation discipline selects a not necessarily full subcategory
\(\Exec\subseteq\Syn_{\mathrm f}\) of well-formed finite open and closed
executable presentations.  Write
\(\iota:\Exec\hookrightarrow\Syn_{\mathrm f}\)
for its inclusion.
Its morphisms preserve types, guarded rule structure, ports, and hierarchical
references.  As part of the discipline, we specify a semantics functor
\[
 \Run:\Exec\longrightarrow\Beh,
\]
where \(\Beh\) may be a category of transition systems, trace presentations,
or another declared behavior category.

This separation is deliberate.  Raw syntax has all finite colimits needed
below.  A particular grammar, type checker, compiler, or scheduler need not be
closed under arbitrary pushout.  All pushouts below are formed in
\(\Syn_{\mathrm f}\).  We call such a pushout \emph{admissible} when its four
objects and all four maps in the pushout square belong to \(\Exec\).  This
condition does not assert that the square is a pushout in the non-full
subcategory \(\Exec\).

\section{Pushout attachments along cofibrations}

\subsection{Interface spans}

The attachment shape is a span
\[
 X\xleftarrow{u}A\xrightarrow{i}B.
\]
Its colimit identifies the two copies of the interface \(A\) and is the
pushout \(X\sqcup_A B\).

In this note, a \emph{cofibration} is a morphism of \(\Syn_{\mathrm f}\) that
is monic in \(\Syn\).  These maps contain the isomorphisms, are closed under
composition, and are stable under pushout.  No weak equivalences, lifting
axioms, or model-category structure are being assumed.

\begin{lemma}[Finite typed pushouts]\label{lem:typed-pushouts}
The category \(\Syn\) is adhesive.  The subcategory \(\Syn_{\mathrm f}\) is
closed under finite colimits.  The class of its morphisms that are
monomorphisms in \(\Syn\) contains the isomorphisms, is closed under
composition, and is stable under pushout.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Presheaf categories are topoi, slices of topoi are topoi, and topoi are
adhesive~\cite{lack,toposes}.  In particular, pushouts along monomorphisms are
also pullbacks, and their cobase changes are monomorphisms.  Colimits in the
presheaf category and its slice are computed from finite coproducts and
quotients componentwise.
Because \(\mathcal C\) has finitely many objects, a finite colimit of pointwise
finite presheaves is again pointwise finite.  The remaining assertions about
isomorphisms and composition are general, while pushout stability follows from
adhesivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Cofibration attachment]
Let \(X,A,B\in\Syn_{\mathrm f}\).  A \emph{cofibration attachment} consists of a
binding \(u:A\to X\) and a boundary inclusion \(i:A\hookrightarrow B\) that is
a cofibration.  Its linked presentation is
\[
 P=X\sqcup_A B.
\]
The attachment is \emph{executable} when this ambient pushout is admissible.
\end{definition}

Here \(A\) is the typed dependency surface, \(B\) is a new rule hierarchy or
module containing that surface, and \(u\) binds its ports to \(X\).  The
boundary map \(i\), rather than the binding \(u\), is required to be monic so
that cobase change preserves the base.

\begin{proposition}[Persistence and exact overlap]\label{prop:persistence}
For a cofibration attachment, the canonical map
\(j_X:X\to P\) is a cofibration, and the canonical comparison
\[
 A\longrightarrow X\times_P B
\]
is an isomorphism.  If the binding \(u\) is also a cofibration, then the
canonical map \(j_B:B\to P\) is a cofibration as well.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The map \(j_X\) is the cobase change of \(i\) along \(u\), so it is a
cofibration.  Because a pushout along a monomorphism in the ambient adhesive
category is a pullback, the canonical map \(A\to X\times_P B\) is invertible.
If \(u\) is a cofibration, its cobase change \(j_B\) is one.
\end{proof}

Thus the base remains componentwise embedded, and the original interface is
recovered as \(X\times_P B\).  If \(u\) is also monic, both pieces embed and
their subobject intersection is precisely \(A\).  The proposition alone does not
imply that \(P\) lies in \(\Exec\); executability is precisely the separate
admissibility hypothesis.  Even for an executable attachment, the universal
property is still ambient unless the inclusion creates this pushout, and
admissibility does not imply that old executions remain successful.

\subsection{The category of attachments}

Fix \(X\in\Syn_{\mathrm f}\).  Let \(\mathbf{Ext}_X\) be the full
subcategory of the coslice \(X\mathbin{\downarrow}\Syn_{\mathrm f}\) whose
objects are cofibrations \(e:X\hookrightarrow Y\).  Let
\(\mathbf{Att}_X\) have objects
\[
 c=(X\xleftarrow{u}A\xrightarrow{i}B)
\]
with \(i\) a cofibration.  For
\(c'=(X\xleftarrow{u'}A'\xrightarrow{i'}B')\), a morphism
\((a,b):c\to c'\) consists of maps \(a:A\to A'\) and \(b:B\to B'\)
satisfying \(u=u'a\) and \(bi=i'a\).

\begin{theorem}[Attachment reflection]\label{thm:attachment-reflection}
After choosing pushouts, linking defines a functor
\[
 L_X:\mathbf{Att}_X\longrightarrow\mathbf{Ext}_X,
 \qquad
 (X\xleftarrow{u}A\xrightarrow{i}B)
 \longmapsto (X\hookrightarrow X\sqcup_A B).
\]
The functor
\[
 D_X:\mathbf{Ext}_X\longrightarrow\mathbf{Att}_X,
 \qquad
 (j:X\hookrightarrow Y)\longmapsto
 (X\xleftarrow{\mathrm{id}_X}X\xrightarrow{j}Y)
\]
is fully faithful and \(L_X\dashv D_X\).  In particular, the counit
\(L_XD_X\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{Ext}_X}\) is an isomorphism, so
\(\mathbf{Ext}_X\) is reflective in \(\mathbf{Att}_X\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A morphism \((a,b):c\to c'\) induces by pushout universality a unique map
\(L_Xc\to L_Xc'\) under \(X\).  Uniqueness gives functoriality, and the
source map of each linked extension is a cofibration by
Proposition~\ref{prop:persistence}.  For
\(e=(j:X\hookrightarrow Y)\), pushout universality gives natural bijections
\[
 \mathbf{Ext}_X(L_Xc,e)
 \cong\{b:B\to Y\mid bi=ju\}
 \cong\mathbf{Att}_X(c,D_Xe).
\]
Thus \(L_X\dashv D_X\).  In a morphism between degenerate attachments the
interface component is forced to be \(\mathrm{id}_X\), so \(D_X\) is fully
faithful; explicitly, it sends a coslice morphism \(h\) to
\((\mathrm{id}_X,h)\).  The pushout of
\(X\xleftarrow{\mathrm{id}_X}X\xrightarrow{j}Y\) is canonically \(Y\),
which gives the counit isomorphism.
\end{proof}

This reflection is ambient.  It restricts to an attachment category internal
to \(\Exec\) only when the presentation discipline is closed under the chosen
attachment pushouts and all induced mediating maps.

\begin{proposition}[Parallel composition]\label{prop:parallel}
The category \(\mathbf{Ext}_X\) has finite coproducts, created by the coslice.
For \(e_s:X\hookrightarrow Y_s\), their coproduct has apex the iterated
pushout \(Y_1\sqcup_X\cdots\sqcup_XY_m\).  If each \(e_s=L_Xc_s\), this apex
is canonically the colimit of the corresponding finite star of attachment
spans.  Thus parallel attachments are independent of ordering up to the
canonical coproduct isomorphisms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identity of \(X\) is initial in \(\mathbf{Ext}_X\).  Binary coproducts in
the coslice are pushouts over \(X\).  The common base map factors as
\(X\hookrightarrow Y_1\hookrightarrow Y_1\sqcup_XY_2\); the second map is a
cobase change of \(X\hookrightarrow Y_2\).  Both factors are cofibrations, so
the coproduct is an object of \(\mathbf{Ext}_X\), and fullness creates it
there.  Iteration gives finite coproducts.  The final claim follows by
comparing the universal properties of the iterated pushout and the star
colimit.
\end{proof}

Cells may also depend on cells attached earlier.  Given
\[
 X_{k-1}\xleftarrow{u_k}A_k\xrightarrow{i_k}B_k,
 \qquad
 X_k=X_{k-1}\sqcup_{A_k}B_k,
\]
with each \(i_k\) a cofibration, repeated application of
Proposition~\ref{prop:persistence} gives a cofibration filtration
\[
 X_0\hookrightarrow X_1\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow X_n.
\]
Every finite stage remains in \(\Syn_{\mathrm f}\).

\section{Execution semantics}

\subsection{The comparison map}

Consider an executable attachment and suppose the behavioral pushout in the
following display exists:
\[
 Q=\Run(X)\sqcup_{\Run(A)}\Run(B).
\]
Applying \(\Run\) to the syntactic pushout cocone produces compatible maps
from \(\Run(X)\) and \(\Run(B)\) to \(\Run(P)\).  Universality therefore gives
a canonical comparison morphism
\[
 \chi:Q\longrightarrow\Run(P).
\]

\begin{definition}[Semantically exact attachment]
An executable attachment is \emph{semantically exact} when \(\chi\) is an
isomorphism.
\end{definition}

Exactness is a sufficient compatibility condition to prove for a language and
linker.  It is not a consequence of the syntactic pushout, and it need not be
necessary for other notions of behavioral preservation.

\begin{theorem}[Semantic monicity criterion]\label{thm:behavioral-persistence}
Assume that the displayed behavioral pushout exists, monomorphisms in
\(\Beh\) are stable under pushout, \(\Run(i):\Run(A)\to\Run(B)\) is monic,
and the attachment is semantically exact.  Then
\[
 \Run(j_X):\Run(X)\longrightarrow\Run(P)
\]
is monic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The canonical map \(\Run(X)\to Q\) is the pushout of \(\Run(i)\), hence is
monic.  The map \(\Run(j_X)\) is its composite with the isomorphism
\(\chi:Q\to\Run(P)\), so it is monic.
\end{proof}

This conclusion is internal to the declared category \(\Beh\).  It entails
literal preservation of old traces or transitions only when the chosen
behavioral morphisms are additionally trace-reflecting or transition-
reflecting.  For a finite chain of attachments satisfying the theorem, the
composite \(\Run(X_0)\to\Run(X_n)\) is monic by closure of monomorphisms under
composition.

The hypotheses expose two independent failure modes.  Compilation may make
the raw pushout inadmissible, for example through a guard or naming conflict.
Even if it compiles, execution may introduce interactions absent from the
behavioral pushout, making \(\chi\) noninvertible.  For example, under a global
``first matching rule wins'' scheduler, attaching a fresh higher-priority rule
can suppress the output formerly selected from \(X\), although the rule-graph
pushout itself is admissible.  Such a scheduler may prevent the proposed
behavior assignment from being functorial on the chosen syntax morphisms.  If
a semantics functor is instead defined in a coarser behavior category, the
comparison map may fail to be invertible.  This simple case rules out inferring
behavioral preservation from syntax alone.  Finite trace enumeration proves
exactness only when the declared semantics is finite and effectively presented
and the comparison isomorphism can be checked exhaustively; finite sampling
does not prove global program equivalence.

\section{Conditional complexity of linking}

Fix a universal prefix machine \(U\) and effective self-delimiting encodings of
finite presentations, morphisms, and tuples.  Write \(\K(z\mid w)\) for
conditional prefix Kolmogorov complexity.  Candidates are encoded as pointed
extensions \((X,j,Y)\), not as independently labeled objects.  Assume that the
base \(X\) has a fixed canonical code.  We canonicalize extensions in the
coslice \(X\mathbin{\downarrow}\Syn_{\mathrm f}\).  Represent \(j\) as a
literal inclusion, retain the fixed labels on its image, and biject the
remaining elements at each sort to a standard finite label set; among the
finitely many incidence codes so obtained, choose the least.  The map to
\(T\) and the marked arrow must be preserved.  The decidability assumptions
on finite attributes make this convention effective.  Complexity therefore
depends on the fixed representation and universal machine, but not on a
gratuitous relabeling outside the base.

For an attachment to \(X\), let
\[
 c=(A,B,u,i)
\]
be its complete attachment code.  Componentwise finite coproducts, quotienting
by the interface identifications, and canonical relabeling give a fixed
computable linker \(\operatorname{Link}\), total on valid finite span codes,
with literal domain \(X\) and
\[
 \operatorname{Link}(X,c)=(j_X:X\hookrightarrow P),
 \qquad P=X\sqcup_A B,
\]
after canonicalization.
The linker computes raw syntax; it need not decide whether its output belongs
to \(\Exec\).  The bounds below are asserted for the admissible outputs.

\begin{proposition}[Computable-gluing bound]\label{prop:gluing-bound}
There is a constant \(b\), depending only on the encodings, universal machine,
and linker, such that every admissible attachment satisfies
\[
 \K((j_X:X\hookrightarrow X\sqcup_A B)\mid X)
 \le \K(A,B,u,i\mid X)+b.
\]
If the cell \(B\) is already available as conditional information, then
\[
 \K((j_X:X\hookrightarrow X\sqcup_A B)\mid X,B)
 \le \K(A,u,i\mid X,B)+b.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A fixed wrapper runs a shortest conditional program for the attachment data
and then invokes \(\operatorname{Link}\).  The wrapper has constant length.
The second inequality uses the same wrapper with \(B\) supplied on the
conditional tape.
\end{proof}

This proposition is the standard monotonicity of prefix complexity under a
fixed partial computable map, applied to the linker.  The first bound does not
supply the cell \(B\) as free side information.  Only the second does so,
because \(B\) is then common conditional information.  More generally, if every candidate may use
one fixed module library \(R\), all complexities and codes must be conditioned
uniformly on \((X,R)\); assigning a different free library to each candidate
would invalidate the comparison.  These are upper bounds, not additive
identities, and shared algorithmic information may shorten a joint code.

For a finite sequence of attachment codes \(c_{1:n}\), iterated linking is
also a fixed partial computable operation.  Writing \(j_{0,n}:X_0\hookrightarrow
X_n\) for the composite pointed extension, one has
\[
 \K((j_{0,n}:X_0\hookrightarrow X_n)\mid X_0)
 \le \K(c_{1:n}\mid X_0)+O(1).
\]
If \(\ell(c_{1:n}\mid X_0)\) is the length of a declared self-delimiting code
with a fixed uniform effective decoder, then
\[
 \K((j_{0,n}:X_0\hookrightarrow X_n)\mid X_0)
 \le \ell(c_{1:n}\mid X_0)+O(1).
\]
The latter is an operational accounting rule.  It should not be reported as
the exact value of \(\K\); the \(O(1)\) term is uniform in \(n\) for the fixed
self-delimiting tuple decoder.

\section{Kolmogorov selection over cofibration extensions}

\subsection{Conditional loss--complexity profiles}

Fixing a universal machine turns Kolmogorov's foundational complexity notion
~\cite{kolmogorov} into a description coordinate.  For finite data \(x\), the
classical structure function is
\[
 h_x(\alpha)
 =\min\{\log_2|S|:x\in S,\ S\text{ finite},\ \K(S)\le\alpha\},
\]
with value \(+\infty\) when the constraint set is empty
~\cite{vereshchagin,livitanyi}.  More general algorithmic rate--distortion
functions replace \(\log |S|\) by a distortion criterion
~\cite{vereshchagin2010}.

Our candidate class is a conditional restriction of that framework.  Fix a
base presentation \(X\in\Exec\), finite data \(D\), and a countable effectively
presented class \(\mathcal E_X\) of morphisms
\(e=(j:X\hookrightarrow Y)\) in \(\Exec\) whose underlying maps are
cofibrations.  The marked arrow is part of a candidate: the same target may
extend \(X\) in different ways.  Let
\[
 L_D(e)\in[0,+\infty]
\]
be a declared execution loss, with infinite loss permitted for nontermination
or failure of a semantic obligation, and put \(k_X(e)=\K(e\mid X)\).

\begin{definition}[Profiles]
For \(\alpha\in\N\) and finite \(r\ge0\), the two conditional
profiles are
\[
 H_{D,X}(\alpha)
 =\min\{L_D(e):e\in\mathcal E_X,\ k_X(e)\le\alpha\}
\]
and
\[
 C_{D,X}(r)
 =\min\{k_X(e):e\in\mathcal E_X,\ L_D(e)\le r\},
\]
with a minimum defined as \(+\infty\) when its constraint set is empty.
\end{definition}

For fixed \(\alpha\), only finitely many candidate encodings have a prefix
description of length at most \(\alpha\), so the first minimum is attained whenever finite.
The second is attained whenever finite because complexity is integer valued.
Consequently, for \(\alpha\in\N\) and finite \(r\),
\[
 H_{D,X}(\alpha)\le r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C_{D,X}(r)\le\alpha.
\]
Both profiles are generally uncomputable ideal quantities.  With empty or
fixed \(X\), candidates equal to finite sets \(S\ni D\), and
\(L_D(S)=\log_2|S|\), the profile \(H_{D,X}\) is the classical structure
function up to the fixed encoding convention.

\subsection{The scalar envelope and MDL specialization}

For \(\lambda>0\), define
\[
 \Phi_{D,X}(\lambda)
 =\inf_{e\in\mathcal E_X}
   \bigl(L_D(e)+\lambda k_X(e)\bigr).
\]
Every infimum over an empty set below is understood to be \(+\infty\).

\begin{proposition}[Exact loss--complexity envelope]\label{prop:envelope}
For every \(\lambda>0\),
\[
 \Phi_{D,X}(\lambda)
 =\inf_{\alpha\in\N}
   \bigl(H_{D,X}(\alpha)+\lambda\alpha\bigr)
 =\inf_{r\ge0}
   \bigl(r+\lambda C_{D,X}(r)\bigr).
\]
If at least one candidate has finite loss, the infimum defining
\(\Phi_{D,X}(\lambda)\) is attained, and every minimizer is Pareto-optimal in
the two coordinates \((L_D,k_X)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any candidate \(e\), set \(\alpha=k_X(e)\).  Then
\(H_{D,X}(\alpha)\le L_D(e)\), so the first right-hand infimum is at most
\(\Phi_{D,X}(\lambda)\).  Conversely, a finite value of
\(H_{D,X}(\alpha)\) is realized by some \(e_\alpha\), and
\[
 \Phi_{D,X}(\lambda)
 \le L_D(e_\alpha)+\lambda k_X(e_\alpha)
 \le H_{D,X}(\alpha)+\lambda\alpha.
\]
Taking infima proves the first equality.  The second follows symmetrically:
use \(r=L_D(e)\) in one direction and a candidate realizing \(C_{D,X}(r)\) in
the other.

For attainment, choose a finite-objective candidate with value \(M\).  Every
candidate with objective at most \(M\) has \(k_X(e)\le M/\lambda\), and there
are only finitely many such candidates.  Their finite set contains a minimizer.
A candidate that weakly
improved both coordinates and strictly improved one would strictly lower the
scalar objective, proving Pareto optimality.
\end{proof}

For a probabilistic specialization, suppose
\(\{P_e(\,\cdot\mid X):e\in\mathcal E_X\}\) is a family of conditional
semimeasures that is uniformly lower semicomputable from \((e,X)\), and set
\(-\log_2 0=+\infty\).  The coding inequality gives
\[
 \K(D\mid e,X)
 \le-\log_2 P_e(D\mid X)+O(1),
\]
and a self-delimiting concatenation therefore gives
\[
 \K(D\mid X)
 \le \K(e\mid X)-\log_2 P_e(D\mid X)+O(1),
\]
where the \(O(1)\) term is uniform in \(D,e,X\) and depends only on the fixed
family decoder.  Thus with \(L_D(e)=-\log_2P_e(D\mid X)\), the value
\(\Phi_{D,X}(1)\) is the ideal conditional two-part MDL optimum; it is finite
exactly when some candidate assigns \(D\) positive mass
~\cite{rissanen,livitanyi}.  This interpretation requires the stated uniform
coding hypothesis; an arbitrary real-valued loss has no such implication.

\subsection{Extension codes versus attachment codes}

Let \(\mathcal C_X\) be the valid codes \(c=(A,B,u,i)\) of admissible
cofibration attachments for which \(e_c=\operatorname{Link}(X,c)\) lies in
\(\mathcal E_X\).  Assume that \(\mathcal E_X\) is closed under the fixed base-preserving
canonicalization and that loss is invariant under pointed isomorphisms fixing
\(X\).  Define the ideal attachment-code profile and scalar objective by
\[
 H^{\mathrm{att}}_{D,X}(\alpha)
 =\min\{L_D(e_c):c\in\mathcal C_X,\ \K(c\mid X)\le\alpha\}
\]
and
\[
 \Phi^{\mathrm{att}}_{D,X}(\lambda)
 =\inf_{c\in\mathcal C_X}
   \bigl(L_D(e_c)+\lambda\K(c\mid X)\bigr).
\]

For the comparison below, choose the object map of \(L_X\) to be the single
canonical computable linker above, uniform in \(X\).  The degenerate
constructor \((X,e)\mapsto D_Xe\) is likewise one uniform computable map.

\begin{theorem}[Attachment--extension coding comparison]\label{thm:attachment-profile}
There are nonnegative integer constants \(b_1,b_2\), depending only on the
fixed machine, encodings, linker, and canonicalizer and independent of
\(D,X,\alpha,\lambda\), such that
\[
 H_{D,X}(\alpha+b_1)
 \le H^{\mathrm{att}}_{D,X}(\alpha),
 \qquad
 \Phi_{D,X}(\lambda)
 \le\Phi^{\mathrm{att}}_{D,X}(\lambda)+\lambda b_1,
\]
and
\[
 H^{\mathrm{att}}_{D,X}(\alpha+b_2)
 \le H_{D,X}(\alpha),
 \qquad
 \Phi^{\mathrm{att}}_{D,X}(\lambda)
 \le\Phi_{D,X}(\lambda)+\lambda b_2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(e=(j:X\hookrightarrow Y)\in\mathcal E_X\), the degenerate attachment
\(D_Xe=(X\xleftarrow{\mathrm{id}_X}X\xrightarrow{j}Y)\) is admissible: its
pushout square has maps \(\mathrm{id}_X,j,j,\mathrm{id}_Y\) in \(\Exec\).
Its canonical link is pointed-isomorphic to \(e\), so closure under
canonicalization puts it in \(\mathcal E_X\) and its code in \(\mathcal C_X\).
The fixed effective object maps of \(L_X\) and \(D_X\) give constants
\[
 \K(L_Xc\mid X)\le\K(c\mid X)+b_1,
 \qquad
 \K(D_Xe\mid X)\le\K(e\mid X)+b_2.
\]
The first is Proposition~\ref{prop:gluing-bound}; the second uses the fixed
degenerate constructor.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:attachment-reflection},
\(L_XD_Xe\cong e\) under \(X\),
so pointed-isomorphism invariance preserves loss.  Applying the two bounds to
the feasible sets and scalar objectives proves all four inequalities.
\end{proof}

The converse deliberately permits degenerate cells.  It shows that unrestricted
attachment syntax has, up to coding constants, no stronger inductive bias than
the extension class itself.  A restricted grammar of primitive cells can
provide a substantive modular bias, but then the converse inequalities need
not hold; the linker bounds remain one-way.

\subsection{Machine dependence and computable proxies}

\begin{proposition}[Invariance]
Hold \(\mathcal E_X\) and \(L_D\) fixed, and let \(U\) and \(V\) be optimal
universal prefix machines.  There is a nonnegative integer \(c\) such that,
for all \(D,X\), \(\alpha\in\N\), finite \(r\ge0\), and \(\lambda>0\),
\[
 H^{U}_{D,X}(\alpha+c)\le H^{V}_{D,X}(\alpha),
 \qquad
 H^{V}_{D,X}(\alpha+c)\le H^{U}_{D,X}(\alpha),
\]
\[
 C^{U}_{D,X}(r)\le C^{V}_{D,X}(r)+c,
 \qquad
 C^{V}_{D,X}(r)\le C^{U}_{D,X}(r)+c,
\]
and, whenever the scalar optima are finite,
\[
 |\Phi^{U}_{D,X}(\lambda)-\Phi^{V}_{D,X}(\lambda)|
 \le\lambda c.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The invariance theorem gives
\(|K_U(e\mid X)-K_V(e\mid X)|\le c\) uniformly in \(e,X\).  Substitution in
the feasibility constraints gives the profile inequalities; substitution in
the scalar objectives and taking infima gives the last one.
\end{proof}

The constant does not make finite-sample choices canonical.  An operational
selector must fix a machine or a computable prefix code.  Let
\(\mathcal C_X^{\mathrm{code}}\) be a declared family of valid codes with one
uniform prefix decoder, and let \(\ell(c\mid X)\) be the explicit code length.
It may minimize
\[
 L_D(\operatorname{Link}(X,c))+\lambda\ell(c\mid X),
 \qquad c\in\mathcal C_X^{\mathrm{code}}.
\]
For an explicitly given finite list of codes, a total linker, and
rational-valued total loss, the minimum is decidable by exhaustive evaluation.
General computable reals may only permit certified interval bounds unless a
separating gap is known; for an enumerable family or partial execution,
anytime search cannot generally certify global optimality.

The explicit length provides an upper bound on the linked extension's prefix
complexity up to a decoder constant.  It nevertheless scores derivations rather than
isomorphism classes: several codes may link to the same extension and thereby
alter the induced prior.  No general approximation guarantee to the ideal
Kolmogorov frontier follows without further assumptions.

\section{Conclusion}

An admissible pushout along a monic boundary preserves the base and recovers
the interface as its exact overlap.  The attachment reflection organizes these
pushouts functorially:
pointed cofibration extensions form a reflective subcategory of attachment
spans, with finite coproducts describing parallel linking.  Semantic exactness
states the additional condition needed for behavioral monicity.  Effective
linking and its fully faithful degenerate-attachment right adjoint give the two
coding directions, so unrestricted attachment and extension codes determine
the same loss--complexity profiles up to uniform additive constants; the
scalar envelope gives regularized selection.

The construction remains conditional.  A concrete executable language must
prove its selected pushouts admissible and its semantics exact or conservative.
A substantive modular prior likewise requires a restricted primitive-cell
grammar excluding the degenerate attachments in the image of \(D_X\).  These
are exactly where language-specific and model-selection results must enter.
