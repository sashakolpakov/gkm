\begin{abstract}
Bongard problems are underdetermined by their small support sets: a fluent
description, an accidental pixel separator, and the intended concept may all
fit the same twelve labelled panels.  The missing layer is therefore not a
theorem prover after prose, but an auditable connection from pixels to
observations.  We define a headless visual-semantic protocol over the complete
12,000-task ShapeBongard V2 release.  Its atomic path first freezes isolated
candidate-independent panel descriptions, proposes affirmative single-phrase
observer predicates from labelled descriptions only, retains a complete
atom-by-panel matrix, and deterministically selects a small positive
conjunction before query release.  An uncalibrated observer nonmatch remains
operational and projects to semantic indeterminacy; it is not certified
absence.  Exact causal receipts and model-free replay make that computation
auditable, while soft prose remains a model observation rather than a proof
about depicted objects.  Python alone defines predicates, the closed IR,
evidence dispositions and projections, calibration, synthesis, selection,
evaluation, persistence, replay, decisions, and every scientific result or
artifact identity.  A checker may only consume an already-frozen Python
artifact and emit a detached, non-authoritative sidecar whose presence,
failure, disagreement, change, or deletion alters none of those values.  The first
Stage-A experiment, A1, failed before scoring and produced no calibration or
accuracy estimate.  A distinct repaired-protocol experiment, A2, was
invalidated by a concurrent agent source edit after protocol/cohort freeze and
wrote no Stage-A terminal artifact.  A3 completed 22 proposer calls and 15
scorer calls, but failed its calibration fit because its upper score bin held
six clusters against a fixed minimum of eight.  Its positive orientation was
13/15 by intended bin versus 2/15 for the exact complement: negation did not
win.  Stage B is unauthorized by A1, A2, and A3.
Official-test model execution is disabled; complete-release authentication
still hashes the test bytes.  The first atomic frame is an N=1
repeated-generator training smoke, not an independent benchmark.  Thus this
paper specifies a protocol and its present representational gaps, not a
positive accuracy result.  Its first live task was consumed by an operational
wrapper failure.  Atomic attempt two then consumed a second task and stopped
after twelve descriptions and one proposal because the prompt required a final
question mark that the shared parser forbade.  Atomic attempt three is a
distinct eight-ID successor and is explicitly pre-live in this manuscript.
\end{abstract}

\section{The theoretical hole}

A Bongard task supplies six positive and six negative support panels, followed
by one held-out panel from each side \cite{bongard1970pattern}.  Many rules can
separate twelve examples.  A proposed rule can exploit renderer artifacts,
generator-family regularities, a model's lexical habits, or extraction failure
instead of the intended visual concept.  Support accuracy alone does not say
which rule was learned.

The tempting account
\begin{equation}
  \text{panel}\longrightarrow\text{verbal description}
  \longrightarrow\text{formal predicate}\longrightarrow\text{proof}
  \label{eq:wrong}
\end{equation}
misplaces the hard problem.  A prover can check consequences of recorded
premises.  It cannot establish from syntax that ``bird-like object'' is a true
description of a PNG.  Formalizing the sentence only makes the unverified
perceptual premise look precise.

The implemented boundary is instead
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{exact panel bytes}
 &\longrightarrow \text{typed empirical observations}\nonumber\\
 &\longrightarrow \text{closed positive formula}
 \longrightarrow \text{support gate and replay}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:actual}
\end{equation}
The first arrow is empirical and must carry provenance, uncertainty, and a
declared failure state.  The later arrows are deterministic Python
computations.  Related visual-language and program-synthesis approaches also
make the representation boundary central \cite{depeweg2018solving,
sonwane2021using}.  A recent Bongard-LOGO diagnostic reports large gains when
pixels are replaced by action programs or structured descriptions, directly
implicating representation rather than formal deduction
\cite{vaishnav2026symbolic}.  Our contribution here is a narrower protocol
contract that does not confuse replay with perceptual truth.

\section{Executable visual semantics}

\subsection{One proposal, candidate-independent observations}

The proposer sees only the labelled support pixels and returns one typed
affirmative proposal: zero to three registered direct atoms and at most one
soft claim containing one to four positive cues.  It cannot return executable
code, feature weights, a threshold, a polarity bit, or a final Boolean.

Independently, each panel's exact PNG bytes are converted to a frozen visual
witness bundle.  The extractor receives no task identifier, side, label,
support/query role, proposed phrase, or formula.  It retains three fixed,
correlated preprocessing scenarios.  A complete conjunction is evaluated
inside each scenario before scenario outcomes are combined; coordinate-wise
feature intervals are not allowed to splice mutually inconsistent witnesses.

Every observation has exactly one disposition:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.1cm,style=nextline]
  \item[\texttt{present}] constructive evidence for the affirmative atom;
  \item[\texttt{certified\_absent}] an operational nonmatch under the frozen
    direct or calibrated contract;
  \item[\texttt{indeterminate}] scenarios disagree or an interval cannot
    decide the fixed boundary; or
  \item[\texttt{error}] extraction, transport, parsing, identity, or replay
    failed.
\end{description}
A failed fit or model call is never interpreted as evidence for the negative
class.  Indeterminate and error outcomes abstain and remain in the headline
denominator.

\subsection{Soft prose as an operational observation}

Open descriptions are still useful proposal language.  For example, a model
may propose ``bird-like object'' or ``mostly oblique parts.''  The atomic
successor to A3 does not promote that text to a theorem:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item twelve isolated vision calls describe one neutrally named support
    panel each, without a task identifier, side, label, other panel, or
    candidate phrase;
  \item a text-only proposer receives those frozen descriptions and support
    labels and emits one to twelve affirmative single-phrase observer atoms;
  \item twelve isolated one-panel observer calls evaluate every atom and
    retain the complete atom-by-panel matrix; and
  \item Python chooses a deterministic positive conjunction of at most four
    atoms and freezes it before either query source is opened.
\end{enumerate}
Two query descriptions and two selected-atom observations then produce a
joint prediction commitment.  Only after that commitment is durably written,
fsynced, reloaded, and decoded are the labels materialized.  Thus a successful
run contains exactly 29 distinct causal model receipts.

Every description binds its exact panel digest, phase, neutral-description
protocol, validated receipt, run commitment, and call ordinal.  Every observer
result additionally binds the scorer producer, version, method, output,
receipt, run, and call ordinal.  This makes the computation over model
observations exact and replayable.  It does not prove bird-likeness.  Recent
symbolic-input diagnostics on Bongard-LOGO likewise locate much of the failure
in pixel-to-representation grounding rather than downstream symbolic
reasoning~\cite{vaishnav2026symbolic}.

The observer vocabulary distinguishes \texttt{operational\_nonmatch} from
semantic absence.  Operational nonmatch may reject a panel only inside an
archive explicitly scoped to that frozen observer.  Its projection into the
general truth lattice is \texttt{indeterminate}, never
\texttt{certified\_absent}.  Such an archive fixes calibration, semantic-truth,
benchmark, and official-test authority to false.  Calibrated-semantic atomic
selection is hard-disabled until Python can cold-validate a typed calibration
artifact and its interval rule.  This is the empirical boundary that the old
minimum-over-cues path blurred.

Whenever possible, development should replace a soft phrase with a direct
measurement.  ``Oblique'' should eventually bind specific corners or stroke
segments and return angle intervals in declared units.  ``Two loops touching''
needs object ownership and a complete contact signature.  This is the relevant
sense in which the predicate library can grow, analogous to reusable library
learning rather than task-local separator invention
\cite{ellis2020dreamcoder,bowers2023stitch}.

\section{Positive-only synthesis and reverse-alignment pathology}

The canonical Python IR contains positive atoms and their conjunction.  The
headless proposer has no \texttt{Not}, complement, disjunction, arbitrary
comparison, or dynamic function lookup.  Every selected atom is used once, and
the verifier assigns its identity.  A proposal must agree with all six positive
and all six negative support panels under fresh isolated evaluation.  The
formula and registry are then frozen before any query pixel is created in the
proposer's workspace.

The earlier PURE support diagnostic recorded 10 reversed outcomes among 132
executable support-panel outcomes.  It did not execute an A1 complement or
measure whether negation improved semantic accuracy.  The observation motivates
a concrete failure mode: if synthesis tries both
$p(x)$ and $\neg p(x)$ after seeing the labels, an incorrectly oriented or
spurious predicate can be rescued by its complement.  If an extractor failure
has first been collapsed to Boolean false, negation turns failure itself into
a positive witness.  With only twelve support panels, family correlations and
reused generator attributes make such accidental separators especially easy
to select.

The repaired protocol fixes the positive orientation at proposal time, has no
negation operation, distinguishes absence from failure, and rejects any rule
that misses the exact 12/12 support gate.  Query observations, joint
predictions, and labels occur only after the formula commitment.  Exact-byte
cold replay then reconstructs the deterministic computation without another
model call.  These restrictions prevent a \texttt{Not} rescue; they do not
make a poor visual vocabulary expressive or turn a support fit into evidence
of generalization.

\section{What formal verification can and cannot add}

Python is the sole authoritative semantics.  Python defines predicate
construction and execution, the closed IR, evidence dispositions and
projections, calibration, synthesis, selection, evaluation, persistence, cold
replay, admission and benchmark decisions, and every scientific result or
artifact identity.  Canonical JSON is the interchange representation.  Lean is
neither imported nor required by the authoritative path.

A checker may consume an already-frozen Python artifact and independently check,
for example, that a conjunction follows from its recorded evidence.  It must be
a detached, removable sidecar:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item no proof term enters a predicate or artifact identity;
  \item installation, change, failure, disagreement, or deletion of the checker
    cannot change a predicate, evidence value, formula, result, benchmark or
    admission decision, digest, identifier, or replay;
  \item the checker cannot alter the canonical Python semantics; and
  \item a proof about recorded observations cannot certify the correctness of
  the original visual observation.
\end{itemize}
This ``un-Lean'' invariant keeps formal checking useful without making the
benchmark depend on a particular prover.  Stage-A source identity enforces the
boundary mechanically: it hashes every potentially authoritative Bongard
Python module but excludes the exact non-authoritative
\nolinkurl{bongard/semantic_checker.py} sidecar, so changing or removing that
adapter leaves receipt identities unchanged.

\section{Limitations and next work}

The protocol makes failures legible, but the central perception problem
remains.  The direct catalog lacks angle, contact, ownership, object identity,
and part-correspondence legs.  The soft scorer may be self-consistent yet
semantically wrong.  A model provider and public-data training history cannot
be reproduced by exact-byte symbolic replay.  One held-out panel per side gives
high task-level variance.  The post-A2 frame has no ledger-disjoint HD DEV unit,
so descriptive BD-only results must not be dressed as broad population
inference.

The atomic implementation now supplies exact affirmative observer questions,
complete panel-wise observations, deterministic small positive conjunctions,
and the absence of \texttt{Not} and polarity flipping.  Its original eligible
frame was deliberately narrow: ten exact-unseen training tasks from already
exposed Basic-shape generators.  Attempt one consumed one.  Attempt two
historically selected from the remaining nine and consumed another, leaving
the exact eight-ID attempt-three frame above.  An N=1 run can test transport and
synthesis, but cannot estimate independent generalization.

The immediate work is richer structured vision.  One prose sentence remains a
lossy bottleneck.  Typed object, part, angle, topology, and relation records
should make visual structure available without task context, while retaining
uncertainty and exact receipt identity.  Calibration must separately recruit
label-blind through a frozen order until bins are powered, or use a fixed cohort
sized for measured attrition and occupancy.  Independently, add the missing
direct observables and redesign the larger evaluation frame.  Only a future,
separately justified design can decide whether official test access is
warranted.

\section{Conclusion}

The project does not need Lean to make a visual predicate executable.  It needs
an honest empirical measurement boundary.  Exact-byte candidate-independent
descriptions and witnesses, explicitly scoped operational observations,
positive-only synthesis, commitment before query release, and Python cold
replay provide such a boundary.  The earlier reversal
diagnostics explain why post-hoc complementation is dangerous; A1 did not
measure negation, while A3 positively observed that the intended orientation
beat its complement.  The positive-only language prevents a complement rescue
from receiving benchmark credit.  Description-to-atom search is now
implemented; the remaining missing legs are richer typed grounding, angles,
point contact, ownership, stable object identity, and a powered evaluation
design.  Until those legs improve, the correct scientific record is one
terminal A1 failure, one invalidated A2 incident with no terminal artifact,
one terminal underpopulated-bin A3 failure, one consumed atomic N=1 operational
wrapper failure with no recoverable score, and one consumed atomic
implementation-contract failure after a cold-replayable 13-call prefix.  The
distinct eight-ID atomic attempt-three successor is PRE-LIVE---there is still
no successful Bongard benchmark.
