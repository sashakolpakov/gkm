\begin{abstract}
A Bongard support set admits many accidental separators, and a vision model's
fluent description is not a proof that the described property is present in
the pixels.  We specify a benchmark protocol that treats those facts as the
central problem.  The protocol inventories all 12,000 official
Bongard-LOGO tasks by content and preserves the released train, validation, and
four-way test split.  The official CLI checks a supplied append-only exposure
ledger and records one task-level event before support release; direct API or
manual reads remain an operational responsibility.  The only task pixels a
headless proposer sees are the labelled support panels, and it
may suggest phrases such as ``bird-like'' or ``contains oblique angles.''  Such
phrases do not execute.  They must be realized by versioned, typed visual legs
whose outputs carry uncertainty and provenance.  A closed positive-only
intermediate representation composes exact leg references; failed extraction
is an abstention or error, never a negative example.  Typed attachment,
nuisance evaluation, candidate replay, normalized implementation-source
binding, and full archive replay are separate admission obligations.  Accepted
legs are grown and glued to the frozen registry only on development tasks.  On
sealed tasks the reusable admitted registry is fixed;
the separate HYBRID baseline still creates one task-local empirical leg after
support.  Its support replay gate and final formula commitment precede query
pixels, predictions precede labels, and model-free replay checks the committed
symbolic computation.  This manuscript is a protocol and systems
specification, not a report of complete-corpus
accuracy.  It states the remaining limits plainly: visual grounding remains
empirical, public-data pretraining contamination cannot be excluded, and formal
verification cannot turn an uncalibrated prose judgment into pixel-level truth.
\end{abstract}

\section{The missing layer}

A Bongard task is a pair
\begin{equation}
  T=(P^+,P^-), \qquad |P^+|=|P^-|=7,
\end{equation}
where the panels in $P^+$ share a rule that the panels in $P^-$ violate
\cite{bongard1970pattern}.  The problem is deliberately underdetermined.  Six
positive and six negative support panels can be separated by semantic rules,
incidental pixel statistics, task identifiers, renderer artifacts, or a broken
extractor that fails more often on one side.  Perfect support accuracy alone
does not distinguish them.

The tempting pipeline
\begin{equation}
  \text{panel}\longrightarrow\text{prose}\longrightarrow
  \text{formal predicate}\longrightarrow\text{proof}
  \label{eq:tempting-pipeline}
\end{equation}
has a gap at its first arrow.  A theorem prover can establish that a formula is
well typed and that its Boolean result follows from supplied observations.  It
cannot establish, from syntax alone, that a vision model's statement
``bird-like object'' is faithful to a raster image.  Calling the prose a
predicate merely hides the perceptual assumption.

Our replacement is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \text{panel bytes}&\longrightarrow
  \text{versioned empirical observations}\\
  &\longrightarrow\text{closed typed formula}
  \longrightarrow\text{empirical gates and replay}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:actual-pipeline}
\end{equation}
The first arrow is empirical.  In the soft-predicate track, calibration is
conditional on externally authenticated scorer and annotation inputs,
preregistration timing, and the stated cross-cluster sampling assumption; the
current HYBRID path instead archives uncalibrated categorical judgments.  The
second arrow is statically checked, and the final arrow combines held-out
evidence with tamper-evident symbolic replay.  No stage is allowed to inherit a
stronger epistemic status than it has earned.

\subsection{Why the negation of a bad predicate can win}

The recurring ``negation wins'' pathology has several concrete causes.
Suppose a search procedure evaluates a score $s(x)$ and, after looking at the
support labels, retains whichever of $s(x)\geq\tau$ and $s(x)<\tau$ scores
better.  It has searched over both a claim and its complement while charging
for one.  On a small support set, an accidental direction is easy to select.
If a detector exception or failed fit is encoded as \texttt{False}, the problem
is worse: detector failure becomes negative evidence, and Boolean negation
turns failure into a positive witness.  Reusing query panels during threshold,
phrase, or polarity selection completes the leak.

The canonical track removes those escape routes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item class orientation is fixed by a verifier-issued support commitment;
  \item the proposer states one positive-class claim and one relation before
  queries exist in its workspace;
  \item the formula language has no \texttt{Not}, polarity bit, inequality
  complement, arbitrary expression, or dynamic code lookup;
  \item missing evidence has four explicit outcomes and cannot silently become
  false; and
  \item selection uses development data, while sealed queries are scored only
  after formula and prediction commitments.
\end{enumerate}
Positive-only syntax does not by itself prevent support overfitting.  Near-miss,
anti-memorization, calibration, nuisance, and sealed-query tests remain
necessary.

labelled oracle audit but never in a visual score.

\subsection{Content identity and structural validation}

The loader accepts both upstream layouts,
\begin{align*}
  &\texttt{\{ff,bd,hd\}/images/task/\{1,0\}/\{0,...,6\}.png},\\
  &\texttt{\{ff,bd,hd\}/png/task/\{1,0\}/\{0,...,6\}.png},
\end{align*}
used respectively by the archive and generator.  It rejects malformed tasks,
duplicate identifiers, unknown split members, overlapping primary splits, and
incorrect official counts.  Each panel is addressed by its byte digest.  A
task manifest hashes its ordered fourteen panel records, and a corpus manifest
hashes the ordered task manifests and normalized split.  Absolute paths are not
part of these content identities.

This boundary matters scientifically.  ``Same task name'' is insufficient if
one image was regenerated, recompressed, or replaced.  Every result must name
the corpus digest, task digest, panel digests, and split source digest against
which it was obtained.

\subsection{Exposure is a second, orthogonal split}

The official split controls comparability; a local exposure ledger contributes
to what this project may call unseen.  In an official CLI run,
\texttt{--require-unseen --cohort} checks exact and semantic collisions and
records one task-level event, with phase, actor, purpose, provenance source,
time, and predecessor digest, before support release.  That event marks all
panels of the task exposed.  Recording produces a new immutable ledger value
and write-once JSON artifact.  Unknown and sealed identifiers fail closed when
the caller supplies the corresponding universes, as the official CLI does.
Direct API or manual reads are not intercepted, and cold verification cannot
authenticate the unavailable history behind a non-empty predecessor ledger.

If prior experiments contaminate part of an official development split, the
remaining eligible task identifiers can be SHA-256 ranked into deterministic
\emph{drill}, \emph{development}, and \emph{sealed confirmation} subsets.
The namespace, counts, membership lists, and partition digest are published.
This local partition never licenses development on the official test set.

``Unseen'' in this manuscript therefore means ``not present in the declared
project exposure ledger for the scoped actors.''  Bongard-LOGO is public.  A
foundation model may have encountered its repository, gallery, paper, or images
during pretraining, and the ledger cannot rule that out.

historically clean: 1,328 Basic tasks drawn from unused generator families and
2,540 Abstract tasks drawn from unused admissible attribute pairs.  All 3,600

local evidence classification, not a theorem that the image bytes were absent

\section{The visual measurement boundary}

\subsection{Candidate-independent observations}

The verifier owns a registry $\Legs_k$ of visual \emph{legs}.  A leg is a
version-pinned callable with a declared input type, output type and unit,
implementation digest, parameters, cost, provenance policy, and behavior under
named transformations.  Typical types include panels, objects, witnesses,
counts, angles in degrees or radians, pixel distances, fractions, and
probabilities.  A formula contains an exact leg name, version, and contract
digest; it cannot ask the runtime to discover a function by string or execute
proposer-supplied code.

The intended admitted-predicate track requires the registry to be frozen before
the support task is issued.  Candidates may then compose those measurements but
cannot create a panel-specific detector after seeing the task.  The generic
episode runner does not enforce that timing; it only validates that the
proposer-returned registry is frozen.  The present headless HYBRID
baseline is deliberately weaker: support pixels produce one task-local prose
claim and model-judgment leg.  Before any query release, twelve fresh isolated
model calls must replay that fixed claim on the support panels with the correct
orientation.  Reversal, mismatch, indeterminacy, or error rejects the proposal;
the runner never negates it.  This is a same-support consistency gate, not
calibration or evidence of generalization, so HYBRID results are reported
separately from admitted-registry results.  New reusable leg code is considered
only in the developmental growth loop described in
Section~\ref{sec:growing-legs}.

There is no universal image normalization.  A leg must declare the typed view
it consumes: \emph{literal ink} when stroke or rendering style is part of the
claim, \emph{carrier shape} when those nuisances may be quotiented out, or a
\emph{relational view} for objects, contact, containment, repetition, symmetry,
and ownership.  A global skeletonization or cleanup step would silently erase
valid concepts that depend on line width, texture, or raster detail.

The implementation now includes one candidate-independent quantitative example:
a bilateral-symmetry leg consumes exact panel bytes, applies a fixed luminance
threshold ensemble and reflection-axis grid, and returns reflected-ink agreement
with a deterministic preprocessing-sensitivity interval.  It preserves all four
evidence dispositions and registers only the affirmative
\texttt{AT\_LEAST} relation.  The interval is not an externally calibrated
confidence interval.  A post-hoc application to v9 is scientifically negative:
multiple negative panels score as more symmetric than positive panels.  Thus the
missing leg is not simply a better threshold on global symmetry.  It must expose
part or lobe ownership, the central junction, and correspondence between the
owned parts.

A separate PURE support-prototype path supplies an executable task-relative
comparison layer without a polarity search.  A candidate-independent extractor
consumes exact PNG bytes at three fixed thresholds and freezes eighteen
interval-valued measurements in four closed groups: topology, global geometry,
moments/symmetry, and boundary/angle.  It receives no task identifier, side
label, prose claim, formula, or query role.  All twelve full support packets are
computed before one Codex call selects exactly one precommitted group.
Support-only fitting then commits positive and negative centroids.  Its sole
contrastive score is
\begin{equation}
  m(x)=d(x,c_{-})-d(x,c_{+}),
\end{equation}
so larger values always mean closer to positive support, and a
development-frozen positive margin defines the predicate.  No operation swaps
the sides after query outcomes.  The adapter performs fresh deterministic
support and query extraction through the generic episode runner; Codex supplies
no code, threshold, weight, polarity, or query observer.

The first fixed twelve-task development calibration falsifies this baseline.
Every group has zero strict support passes.  The best group obtains only 10/24
development images and 2/12 puzzles; one border-clipped support panel remains
in every denominator as an unfittable-support error.  The failure is
representational: coordinate-wise interval boxes discard correlation among
preprocessing scenarios, one centroid assumes a convex unimodal class, the
proposal language permits only one group, and global raster statistics omit
parts, ownership, correspondence, signed curvature, and open semantic
concepts.  The proposal prose is hash-bound but not load-bearing evidence; the
compiled atom proves only an operational match to the frozen support
prototype.  This development result is not an official benchmark score.

\subsection{Four evidence dispositions}

Every leg invocation yields exactly one of
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.1cm,style=nextline]
  \item[present] a value and provenance, optionally with an uncertainty
  interval;
  \item[certified absence] no value, plus a certificate valid relative to the
  leg contract;
  \item[indeterminate] insufficient evidence and an explicit reason; or
  \item[error] a failed computation with an error type and reason.
\end{description}
A failed fit, missing contour, timeout, malformed response, or uncalibrated
score is therefore not a negative prediction.  Only present formula evidence
predicts the positive class; only certified formula absence predicts the
negative class.  Indeterminate and error outcomes abstain and count against
unconditional benchmark performance.

The generic HYBRID model observer reports an empirical \texttt{nonmatch}, not
pixel-level certified absence.  The archive can mechanically certify only that
the frozen model procedure emitted that nonmatch for the operational claim.
Calling the depicted property absent requires a dedicated registered certifier
or a calibrated predictive interval wholly below its frozen threshold.

The episode withholds exactly one positive and one negative panel.  If the
caller-supplied seed makes query order uniform and independent of the
predictor, a no-vision policy that assigns opposite labels to the two opaque
slots attains 50\% expected puzzle and image accuracy.  Puzzle accuracy then
does not have a 25\% independent-label baseline.  The code does not enforce
seed randomness, secrecy, or external preregistration, and cold verification
cannot recompute the schedule from the seed digest.  We report this conditional
50\% baseline together with per-image and per-side accuracy across
independently selected semantic groups.

\subsection{Soft predicates grounded in vision}

A vision proposer may say ``bird-like object,'' ``mostly oblique angles,'' or
``two loops touching at one point.''  The sentence is useful for discovery,
but prose is not executable evidence.  A complete soft semantic path for
phrase $\phi$ requires two distinct legs,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    v_\phi &: \mathrm{Panel}\longrightarrow\mathrm{FrozenVisualScore},\\
    c_\phi &: \mathrm{FrozenVisualScore}\longrightarrow\\[-0.25em]
           &\quad
             \mathrm{Evidence}\bigl(
               \mathrm{CalibratedPredictiveSupport}[0,1]
             \bigr).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first leg is the empirical pixels-to-score operation and must bind the
exact panel bytes, model, prompt, decoder, implementation, and raw receipt.  The
current development module does not implement or authenticate this leg; it
begins from an externally admitted frozen-score packet.  Given a caller-supplied
expected plan digest, the second leg maps that packet to a bin-level predictive
interval $[l,u]$ using exact declared development groups, annotation protocol,
population, sampling design, and scorer identity.  The module checks those
content identities and prevents a task, generator group, or model call from
being split across dependence clusters.  It does not authenticate receipts or
publication time, nor establish independence between clusters; the Hoeffding
bounds are valid only if the outer sampling design justifies that assumption.
A batch of correlated judgments from one model call is one replication unit,
not many independent votes.  The interval is predictive support for the
registered population, not an interval for an individual panel's metaphysical
semantic truth.

For an atom $\ell_\phi(x)\geq\tau$, interval-safe evaluation gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{cases}
  \text{present}, & l\geq\tau,\\
  \text{certified absent relative to the contract}, & u<\tau,\\
  \text{indeterminate}, & l<\tau\leq u.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:interval-decision}
\end{equation}
The threshold and direction are frozen with the positive-class claim; the
runner does not try both polarities and keep the winner.

Soft does not mean arbitrary.  ``Bird-like'' remains an empirical category and
must retain that label.  ``Oblique angles'' can often be sharpened: vision may
bind candidate strokes or corners, after which a deterministic leg returns an
angle interval in degrees.  The growth process should prefer this transition
from a free phrase to a typed witness and measurement whenever the pixels
support it.  The two-loop point-contact example similarly needs explicit loop
owners, a contact witness, incident rays, exterior gaps, uncertainty, and
provenance; a successful contour fit alone is not that signature.

\section{Closed positive-only predicate IR}

The canonical intermediate representation $\IR$ contains three formula nodes:
\begin{equation}
  F ::= \mathrm{Atom}\mid \mathrm{AllOf}(F_1,\ldots,F_m)
      \mid \mathrm{AnyOf}(F_1,\ldots,F_m).
\end{equation}
The general proposal schema can represent positive atoms and conjunctions, but
the canonical headless HYBRID CLI supplies no observable catalog and requires
exactly one positive \texttt{hybrid\_claim} atom.  \texttt{AnyOf} remains
available for verifier-owned retained-library formulas with an explicit
semantic justification; the canonical proposer cannot synthesize it.
An atom is an exact static leg call with one of four declared relations:
\texttt{present}, \texttt{at\_least}, \texttt{at\_most}, or
\texttt{between}.  Scalar thresholds must have the leg's unit.  An
\texttt{at\_most} claim is admitted for an intrinsically small-valued positive
property such as low closure error; it is not a license to test both directions
of an unrelated score after seeing labels.  Conjunctions and disjunctions carry
an explicit semantic justification.

Formally, each leg contract contains a signed set of affirmative relations
$A_\ell$.  A scalar call is well formed only when its relation is a member of
$A_\ell\subseteq\{\texttt{at\_least},\texttt{at\_most},\texttt{between}\}$;
a non-scalar witness leg admits only \texttt{present}.  This
\texttt{AffirmativeRelation} set is fixed and digest-bound when the leg is
registered.  It is not a parameter that synthesis may fit.  Consequently, a
feature registered as increasing evidence cannot win by an undeclared
\texttt{at\_most} rewrite, even if that rewrite happens to separate the labeled
support panels.

There is no negation node, post-hoc polarity flag, not-equal relation, Python
expression, import, callback, or dynamic leg lookup.  The compiler typechecks
every boundary argument, parameter, unit, registry version, and contract digest
before execution.  Composite evaluation preserves the four evidence states;
exceptions and uncertainty propagate rather than being coerced to Boolean
false.

The reference typechecker, evaluator, and cold replay are implemented in pure
Python.  Their input contract---the serialized formula, registry snapshot, and
four-disposition evidence---does not contain Python source or proof-assistant
terms.  Thus Python is sufficient for both benchmark execution and
tamper-detecting replay.

This division also gives a precise optional formal-verification target.  A
proof assistant could independently verify grammar closure, type preservation,
interval comparison, evidence propagation, and the theorem
\begin{equation}
  \text{committed observations}\vdash_{\IR}\text{committed prediction}.
\end{equation}
It could not prove the premise that a particular panel is bird-like without a
formal image model or a trusted empirical axiom for the visual leg.  Lean is
therefore neither required nor currently used; any future Lean checker must be
removable without changing formula identities, admission, or replay.  The
current Python cold replay checks the symbolic implication and artifact
integrity; it does not re-establish perceptual correctness.

Removability is an explicit portability objective.  Python predicates are the
reference executable semantics; adding and later ``un-Leaning'' an optional
checker must not translate the leg library or invalidate historical artifacts.

\section{Three distinct contracts}

Three obligations are easy to blur and must remain separate.

\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
\toprule
Contract & What it establishes & What it does not establish \\
\midrule
Typed attachment & The formula plugs into an exact frozen registry through
declared types, units, parameters, versions, and digests. & That a leg measures
the intended visual property or generalizes under rendering changes. \\
Nuisance evaluation & The candidate empirically preserves or transforms its
decision as declared under required translation, rotation, reflection, scale,
stroke-width, resolution, or style trials. & Static type safety, semantic
uniqueness, calibration on new concepts, or preservation of earlier legs. \\
Archive preservation & Every previously accepted attachment cold-replays under

\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Independent obligations for admitting a visual leg or composite.}
\label{tab:contracts}
\end{table}

Admission additionally checks soft-score calibration against a baseline,
near-miss contrasts, anti-memorization challenges, zero train/query content
overlap, forbidden identifier access, candidate replay, and a source-structure
novelty charge.  The SHA-256 of stripped submitted candidate source must match
the registered leg's stripped \texttt{inspect.getsource} digest, or its selected
bytecode-tuple fallback, and the formula must directly call that leg; the
comparison source must match a verifier precommit.  This binds normalized
source or bytecode identity, not dependencies, globals, closure contents,
package environment, or machine code.  Thus harmless decoy text cannot purchase
a cheap novelty score for different registered code.  All receipts bind the same
candidate digest and verifier identity.  The current receipt structures are
data contracts, not cryptographic signatures, so production evaluation must
construct them inside the verifier-owned process or authenticate them
externally.  Promotion is atomic: a rejected candidate cannot partially mutate
the registry or archive, and an accepted decision applies only if the archive
has not changed since evaluation.

\section{Growing legs by cofibrant-style gluing}
\label{sec:growing-legs}

A fixed vocabulary merely moves the hand-engineering problem.  The development
system therefore alternates two loops.

\paragraph{Composition loop.}
Given a drill task and frozen $\Legs_k$, the headless proposer describes the
positive side, inspects counterexamples, and emits only a formula over registered
leg references.  The verifier fits permitted parameters on support, evaluates
development panels, and returns typed counterexamples.  If a composition works,
no new leg is needed.

\paragraph{Growth loop.}
If the current registry cannot express a stable solution, the proposer may
stage one new leg or refactoring.  Its boundary $A_k$ consists of the declared
input and output types, units, parameters, invariance obligations, provenance,
and cost.  Its implementation and verifier-owned tests form a cell $B_k$.
The map $A_k\to\Legs_k$ states how the cell attaches to the existing registry.
After all gates pass, the operational pushout analogue is
\begin{equation}
  \Legs_{k+1}=\Legs_k\cup_{A_k}B_k.
  \label{eq:gluing}
\end{equation}
This ``cofibrant-style'' language is an engineering discipline, not a claim
that the Python implementation proves a categorical universal property.  The
important properties are explicit boundary data, least compatible extension,
and replay of the full incumbent archive.  Repeated successful compositions can
be factored into a shared derived leg; one-off task code is not promoted merely
because it separates a support set.

The outer objective may rank gate-passing candidates by
\begin{equation}
  F(h)=R_{\mathrm{drill/dev}}(h)+\lambda C(h),
  \label{eq:free-energy}
\end{equation}
where $C$ charges leg source structure, contracts, calls, bindings, and
parameters.  Description-length pressure is useful for choosing among
empirically admissible candidates \cite{rissanen1978modeling,
grunwald2007minimum}.  It is not a semantics test and cannot create novelty.
The proposer supplies novelty; the verifier decides whether the new cell earns
attachment.  This resembles library learning and continual problem invention
\cite{ellis2020dreamcoder,bowers2023stitch,schmidhuber2013powerplay}, but the
present protocol insists on a visual measurement boundary and full archive
replay.

No reusable-leg growth, threshold fitting, phrase revision, or admitted-registry
mutation occurs during official sealed evaluation.  The HYBRID baseline remains
separate because it constructs one task-local empirical leg after support.

\section{Sealed headless-proposer evaluation}

The canonical run is a verifier-controlled commit/release sequence.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Preregister.}  The intended outer protocol externally commits
  corpus and exposure-ledger heads, official and local partition digests,
  proposer/model and prompt versions, reusable registry and archive digests,
  support/query schedule, nuisance suite, scoring rules, and resource limits.
  The current CLI content-addresses its plan and writes the exposure event
  before support, but does not prove external publication time or that the
  supplied ledger head is latest; HYBRID's task-local registry is compiled only
  after support.
  \item \textbf{Issue support.}  Copy six positive and six negative panels into
  a fresh sandbox under canonical labelled names \texttt{pos\_0.png} through
  \texttt{neg\_5.png}.  Commit their bytes and labels.  The headless proposer
  has no access to corpus paths, official task identifiers, action programs,
  query store, labels outside support, or prior sealed artifacts.
  \item \textbf{Fix and compile the proposal.}  The proposer returns one
  positive-class description.  Verifier-owned code compiles the canonical
  HYBRID path into one task-local atom and frozen registry.  A generic proposer
  need only return a registry that is already frozen; the runner does not prove
  it was frozen before support.
  \item \textbf{Replay support and freeze.}  Make twelve fresh isolated calls,
  each with the fixed claim and one support panel named \texttt{query.png}, and
  join the hidden labels inside the verifier.  Reject unless every positive is
  present and every negative is operationally certified absent.  The final
  proposal freeze binds the complete support-gate digest.
  \item \textbf{Release queries.}  Only after the accepted gate and final
  freeze, release the remaining
  two distinct query panels without labels.  Run registered legs, retaining
  atom-level evidence, uncertainty, and provenance.
  \item \textbf{Commit predictions.}  Reduce the evidence through the frozen
  formula.  Commit both predictions or abstentions and their evidence digests.
  \item \textbf{Reveal labels.}  Release the two labels only after the
  prediction commitment.  The task-level exposure event written before support
  already marks every panel of this task exposed; later disclosures are not
  separate ledger events in the current CLI.
  \item \textbf{Verify and cold-replay.}  Check an externally anchored raw
  run-file digest, the trusted release descriptor, split assignment, task
  manifest, a bijection to all fourteen official panel byte preimages, the
  support gate, and each vision receipt's internal request/response bindings.
  Reparse the proposal and canonically recompile the verifier-owned HYBRID
  registry, formula, source and operational identities, and attachment contract
  without invoking the model; then replay committed atom dispositions.  The
  receipts are not provider signatures, and re-running perceptual legs from
  panel bytes is a separate warm-replay audit.
\end{enumerate}

The chain is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \text{support}
      &\to\text{fixed claim}\to\text{support replay gate}\\
      &\to\text{final proposal freeze}\to\text{query release}\\
      &\to\text{cold inputs}\to\text{predictions}\to\text{labels},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with every artifact containing or being bound by its parent's digest.  This
establishes temporal ordering and tamper evidence.  It does not establish that
the caller-supplied seed was random or preregistered, that a non-empty exposure
predecessor is the latest authentic ledger head, or that the operating system,
remote model provider, or model pretraining corpus contained no side channel;
those are additional deployment assumptions that must be reported.

\section{Evaluation and falsification}

The primary unit is a task, not a panel or an API response.  A task is exactly
correct only when both held-out queries receive determinate, correct labels.
The report must include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item exact-task accuracy and unconditional panel accuracy, counting
  abstentions as failures;
  \item coverage, conditional accuracy on determinate predictions, and the
  complete four-disposition table;
  \item results separately for FF, BA, CM, and NV, with uncertainty resampled
  by task;
  \item Brier score and reliability information for soft semantic legs;
  \item proposer calls, latency, token or compute use, registry size, new-leg
  attachments, rejected attachments, novelty charges, and archive regressions;
  and
  \item preregistered results separately from post-hoc diagnostics.  A useful
  predicate discovered after query labels are visible earns no benchmark
  credit.
\end{itemize}

Necessary controls include direct headless vision classification, prose without
executable grounding, the frozen deterministic-leg registry without soft
semantics, soft semantics without leg growth, the full growing-leg system, and
an unrestricted complement/negation diagnostic.  Action-program and official
rule metadata form an explicitly non-visual oracle arm.  Nuisance, near-miss,
label-shuffle, identifier-leak, and archive-replay ablations test different
failure modes and must not be collapsed into one score.

The protocol is falsified, rather than rescued, when a candidate needs query
feedback, succeeds only after polarity reversal, converts errors to negatives,
uses official identifiers, fails required nuisance trials, loses calibration,
memorizes panel bytes, or breaks any accepted archive entry.

\section{Implementation boundary}

The canonical package implements the following contracts:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item official-layout discovery, exact task and split validation, and
  content-addressed panel/task/corpus manifests;
  \item immutable exposure ledgers, exact and semantic unseen checks,
  deterministic partitions for drill, development, and sealed use, and
  historical exposure imports; the official CLI applies these checks to its
  supplied ledger head, but direct reads and predecessor authenticity remain
  outside that mechanism;
  \item typed, version-pinned leg contracts with units, transformation
  declarations, signed affirmative relations, implementation digests, and a
  verifier-owned frozen registry; the reusable pre-support registry discipline
  remains distinct from the post-support task-local HYBRID registry;
  \item a deterministic candidate-independent bilateral-symmetry leg, and a
  support-prototype core with neutral interval feature packets, committed
  positive and negative centroids, and one fixed positive contrastive margin;
  neither is yet an externally calibrated official-runner vision solution;
  \item provenance-bearing four-state evidence, soft semantic observations, and
  a conditional score-to-predictive-support calibration bridge;
  \item the closed positive-only formula IR and interval-safe evaluator;
  \item typed attachment, calibration, nuisance, near-miss,
  anti-memorization, candidate replay, normalized implementation-source
  binding, novelty, and
  archive-preservation admission gates; and
  \item immutable support/gate/freeze/query/prediction/label artifacts,
  verifier-owned canonical HYBRID recompilation, and model-free replay.
\end{itemize}

These are executable and testable protocol components.  They are not, by
themselves, a completed end-to-end benchmark, a trained visual vocabulary, or
evidence of high Bongard accuracy.  Earlier exploratory runs under different
datasets, latent generators, mutable candidate vocabularies, or post-hoc
analysis are development history and are not results of this protocol.

\section{Limitations}

The remaining limitations are substantive.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Soft visual semantics can be calibrated empirical measurements, not
  mathematical definitions of bird-likeness or other open phrases.  The current
  bridge starts after scoring, authenticates neither scorer nor annotations,
  and relies on an outer cross-cluster sampling assumption.
  \item A cold replay verifies committed symbolic evaluation but trusts the
  committed perceptual observations.  Independent warm replay and provider
  reproducibility remain necessary.
  \item Synthetic nuisance transformations test declared changes, not every
  semantic alternative or real renderer shift.
  \item With one query per class, a single task has very high variance.  Claims
  require many sealed tasks and task-level uncertainty.
  \item The official NV label is a property of the released dataset split.  It
  does not prove novelty to a foundation model or to this project's prior
  conceptual vocabulary.
  \item The public corpus and galleries may be present in model pretraining.
  Local exposure accounting supports procedural honesty, not a global
  non-contamination theorem.
  \item Positive-only syntax removes a major rescue path but cannot make an
  impoverished visual registry expressive.  Honest abstention and subsequent
  development-time leg growth are expected outcomes.
  \item The cofibrant-gluing analogy guides modular admission; no proof
  assistant currently certifies the Python implementation or its category-level
  universal properties.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

The missing component in Bongard predicate synthesis is not another separator
search.  It is a defensible boundary between pixels and claims.  Vision may
propose human-scale phrases, including soft ones.  In the calibrated soft track,
executable use must pass through a typed, provenance-bearing predictive-support
observation whose validity is conditional on authenticated inputs and sampling
assumptions.  The current HYBRID baseline instead uses task-local categorical
judgments plus support replay and is uncalibrated.  Closed positive composition
prevents complement rescue; four-state evidence prevents
failed extraction from masquerading as absence; separate attachment,
invariance, and archive contracts prevent one successful check from standing in
for all others.  Growing legs can then expand the vocabulary on development
tasks without permitting sealed-task invention, and the official 12,000-task
corpus provides enough room to drill, validate, and retain a real sealed test.

The immediate scientific deliverable is therefore a preregistered headless
proposer run over the complete corpus, reported with coverage, calibration,
per-regime results, exposure provenance, and both cold and perceptual replay.
Until that run exists, this work should be cited as a protocol and
implementation, not as a successful Bongard benchmark result.
